## What Quality Score Is

Quality Score is a **1 to 10 rating** shown at keyword level. It estimates how relevant and useful your ad and landing page are for that keyword.

### Important truths about it

**1. It is a diagnostic, not the real number.** The auction uses a live, more detailed quality signal calculated fresh for every auction. The 1–10 score is a simplified summary shown to you.

**2. It is based on exact match history.** Your Quality Score for a keyword reflects performance when the keyword matched searches identical or near-identical to it — not all the broad match traffic it picked up.

**3. It updates slowly.** Changes take days or weeks to show. Do not check it daily.

**4. Low-volume keywords show a null score** (a dash, or 6 by default). There is not enough data. Ignore these.

So: **do not obsess over the number. Do care intensely about what it measures.**

---

## The Three Components

Each keyword's Quality Score is built from three ratings: Below average, Average, or Above average.

### 1. Expected CTR
Google's prediction of whether people will click your ad for this keyword.

**Driven by:** your historical CTR for that keyword, your ad copy strength, your assets, your position.

### 2. Ad Relevance
How closely your ad matches the intent of the keyword.

**Driven by:** whether your ad text reflects what the keyword means, and whether the keyword belongs in that ad group at all.

### 3. Landing Page Experience
How useful, relevant, fast and honest your landing page is.

**Driven by:** message match, page speed, mobile usability, original content, transparency, navigation.

## What Quality Score Is Actually Worth

Let's put real money on it. Use the cost formula from Lesson 1.1.

Assume the competitor below you has an Ad Rank of 300.

| Your Quality Score | Your cost per click |
|---|---|
| 3 | PKR 100 |
| 5 | PKR 60 |
| 7 | PKR 43 |
| 8 | PKR 37.50 |
| 10 | PKR 30 |

**Moving from 5 to 8 cuts your cost per click by 37.5%.**

### What that means for a real account

Sana spends PKR 250,000 a month at an average CPC of PKR 40, with an average Quality Score of 5.

If she raises the average to 8:
- New effective CPC: about PKR 25
- Same budget now buys **10,000 clicks instead of 6,250**
- At a 2.4% conversion rate: **240 sales instead of 150**
- At PKR 2,600 margin: **PKR 234,000 extra profit per month**

**Same spend. Same ads running. 60% more sales.**

This is why Quality Score work is not optional detail. It is one of the largest levers available.

⚠️ **A caution on this maths:** it assumes you can buy those extra clicks at that price, which depends on available impression volume. In a small market you may hit a ceiling. Still, the direction is real and large.

## How to See Your Quality Score

**Add these columns** in the Keywords view (Columns → Modify columns → Quality Score):

- Quality Score
- Exp. CTR
- Ad relevance
- Landing page exp.

**Also add the historical versions:**
- Qual. Score (hist.)
- Landing page exp. (hist.)
- Ad relevance (hist.)
- Exp. CTR (hist.)

The historical columns let you segment by date and see whether your changes actually improved anything. Without them, you only ever see today's snapshot.

## Fixing Expected CTR

**Symptom:** "Below average" on Expected CTR.

### What to do, in order of impact

**1. Rewrite the ad using Lesson 4.3.**
Replace adjectives with specifics. Add numbers. Add the mechanism. This is usually the biggest fix.

**2. Include the keyword in the headlines.**
At least three of your 15 headlines should contain the keyword's actual words (Lesson 4.1, slots 1 and 2).

**3. Build the full asset set (Lesson 4.2).**
Sitelinks, callouts, images. A physically bigger ad gets clicked more.

**4. Check your position.**
Ads at the bottom of the page get fewer clicks. Sometimes low expected CTR is a symptom of low Ad Rank, not the cause. Check impression share lost to rank.

**5. Move the keyword to a tighter cluster.**
If the keyword sits in an ad group whose ads do not really speak to it, the ad can never earn a good CTR for it (Lesson 3.5).

**6. Consider pausing it.**
Some keywords have really low intent and will never earn a good CTR. That is a keyword selection problem, not a copy problem.

---

## Fixing Ad Relevance

**Symptom:** "Below average" on Ad Relevance.

This is usually the **easiest and fastest** component to fix.

### What to do

**1. Check the keyword actually belongs in this ad group.**
This is the most common cause. A keyword about implants sitting in a general dentistry ad group will never show good relevance. Move it (Lesson 3.5).

**2. Use the keyword's words in the ad.**
Not forced, but present. Google is literally checking whether your ad is about the thing the keyword is about.

**3. Tighten the ad group theme.**
If your ad group covers five different intents, no single ad can be relevant to all of them. Split it.

**4. Check for duplicate keywords across ad groups.**
Two ad groups competing for the same keyword confuses both relevance and reporting.

**Typical time to improve:** 1–2 weeks after the change.

---

## Fixing Landing Page Experience

**Symptom:** "Below average" on Landing Page Experience.

This is the **slowest to fix** but often the most valuable, because it also raises conversion rate.

### What to do

Everything in Lesson 0.5, plus:

**1. Message match.** The page headline must repeat the ad's promise.

**2. Speed.** Test on mobile, on mobile data. Target under 2.5 seconds for the main content. Compress images first.

**3. Mobile usability.** Real thumbs, real phone. Buttons big enough. No horizontal scrolling.

**4. Original, useful content.** Thin pages with 40 words of text score badly. So do pages that are mostly a form.

**5. Transparency.** Business details, contact information, privacy policy, refund policy. This overlaps directly with the misrepresentation policy (Lesson 1.5).

**6. Easy navigation to what was promised.** If the ad promises prices, the visitor should see prices without hunting.

**7. Dedicated pages per cluster.** Sending five different intents to one homepage guarantees poor scores for at least four of them.

**Typical time to improve:** 2–6 weeks. Google needs to re-crawl and gather new behaviour data.

## Realistic Expectations

You cannot get 10/10 on everything, and trying will waste your time.

### Where high scores are achievable

| Keyword type | Realistic Quality Score |
|---|---|
| Your own brand terms | 9–10 |
| Tight, high-intent keywords with a matching page | 7–9 |
| Broad category terms | 5–7 |
| Competitor brand terms | 2–4 (and this is normal) |
| Loosely related discovery terms | 3–5 |

**Competitor conquesting keywords will always have a low Quality Score.** Your ad cannot be highly relevant to someone else's brand name, and your page is not about them. That is expected, and it is priced into the strategy (Lesson 9.6).

### Where to spend your effort

Sort keywords by **cost**, not by Quality Score.

A keyword with a Quality Score of 3 that spends PKR 200 a month is not worth an hour of your time. A keyword with a Quality Score of 6 that spends PKR 60,000 a month is worth a whole afternoon.

**Fix the expensive ones first.**

## The 30-Day Quality Score Improvement Plan

### Week 1: Diagnose
- Add all Quality Score columns, including historical
- Export keywords with cost, Quality Score and all three components
- Sort by cost, highest first
- Take your top 20 by spend. For each, note which components are Below average
- Count which component appears most often. That is your priority.

### Week 2: Ad Relevance and Expected CTR
- Move any keyword that is in the wrong ad group
- Rewrite ads for your top 5 spending ad groups using the Lesson 4.1 framework
- Build the full asset set for those campaigns (Lesson 4.2)
- Add negatives for irrelevant search terms hurting CTR (Lesson 3.4)

### Week 3: Landing Page Experience
- Run PageSpeed Insights on the top 5 landing pages, mobile
- Compress images and remove unused scripts
- Fix message match on all five headlines
- Confirm business details, contact info and policies are present

### Week 4: Measure and repeat
- Segment your historical Quality Score columns by week
- Compare week 1 to week 4 for the keywords you worked on
- Calculate the CPC change and the money saved
- Find the next 20 keywords by spend and repeat

**Do not expect instant results.** Expected CTR and Ad Relevance move in 1–2 weeks. Landing Page Experience can take 4–6.

## 📖 Real-World Example: The 90-Day Quality Score Project

Bilal took over a US B2B services account. Spend: $18,000 a month. Cost per lead: $340. The client thought their market was simply expensive.

**The diagnosis.** He exported the keywords and sorted by cost. The top 25 keywords carried 71% of spend. Here is what he found:

| Component | "Below average" count in top 25 |
|---|---|
| Expected CTR | 19 |
| Ad Relevance | 22 |
| Landing Page Experience | 25 |

**All 25 top-spending keywords had a below-average landing page.** Every one of them pointed to the homepage.

Average Quality Score across the top 25: **4.2**.

### What he did, in three phases

**Phase 1 (weeks 1–2): Ad Relevance.**
He found 11 keywords sitting in ad groups that did not match them. He rebuilt the structure into six intent clusters (Lesson 3.5) and moved every keyword to where it belonged. Then he wrote a new RSA for each cluster using the 15-slot framework, ensuring the keyword's actual words appeared in at least three headlines.

**Phase 2 (weeks 2–4): Expected CTR.**
Full asset build: 8 sitelinks per campaign with descriptions, 10 callouts, 3 structured snippets, 12 images. He also added 140 negatives from an n-gram analysis, removing irrelevant traffic that had been dragging CTR down.

**Phase 3 (weeks 4–10): Landing Page Experience.**
This was the slow part. He convinced the client to build **six dedicated landing pages**, one per cluster. Each one:
- Repeated the ad headline exactly
- Loaded in under 2.5 seconds on mobile
- Had the business address, phone, privacy policy and terms visible
- Had a single call to action, no navigation menu

The client's developer took three weeks. Bilal spent that time writing the page briefs (the Expert Challenge from Lesson 0.5).

### Results at 90 days

| Metric | Day 0 | Day 90 |
|---|---|---|
| Average Quality Score (top 25) | 4.2 | 8.1 |
| Average CPC | $14.20 | $8.40 |
| Clicks per month | 1,268 | 2,143 |
| Conversion rate | 4.2% | 6.9% |
| Leads per month | 53 | 148 |
| Cost per lead | $340 | $122 |
| Monthly spend | $18,000 | $18,000 |

**Same budget. 179% more leads.**

### Where the improvement actually came from

This is worth breaking down, because it shows how the effects compound:

1. **Lower CPC (from Quality Score)** bought 69% more clicks for the same money.
2. **Better landing pages** raised conversion rate by 64%.
3. **Those two multiply**: 1.69 × 1.64 = **2.77×** — which matches the 179% increase almost exactly.

**The landing page work did both jobs at once.** It raised Quality Score (lowering cost per click) *and* raised conversion rate (getting more from each click). That dual effect is why Landing Page Experience is the slowest component to fix and the most valuable.

**The client's reaction:** *"So the market was never expensive. We were just expensive to Google."*

## 📌 Key Takeaways

- Quality Score is a diagnostic. The number matters less than the three components underneath it.
- Higher quality directly lowers your cost per click. Going from 5 to 8 cuts CPC by around 37%.
- Fix Ad Relevance first — it is fastest. Fix Landing Page Experience last — it is slowest and most valuable.
- Sort by cost, not by score. Fix the expensive keywords, ignore the cheap ones.
- Landing page work raises Quality Score and conversion rate at the same time, and those effects multiply.

## 🎯 Expert Challenge

Build a Quality Score improvement programme with proper measurement.

- Set up the baseline using historical Quality Score columns segmented by week, so you can prove change rather than assert it.
- Design the intervention sequence so that changes are separated enough in time to attribute the effect. Changing ads and pages in the same week means you learn nothing about which worked.
- Build the financial model: for each component improvement, estimate the CPC change, the extra clicks, the extra conversions and the profit. Present this as the business case before starting.
- Find the keywords where high Quality Score is structurally impossible, and exclude them from the programme with a written explanation. Chasing these wastes your time and damages your credibility.
- After 90 days, produce the results analysis separating Quality Score effects (cheaper clicks) from conversion rate effects (better clicks). Most people cannot do this, and it is the difference between "results improved" and "here is exactly why".
