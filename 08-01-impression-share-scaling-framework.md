## What Impression Share Measures

**Impression share (IS)** is the percentage of times your ad appeared, out of the times it *could* have appeared.

> **IS = Impressions you got ÷ Impressions you were eligible for**

If your IS is 40%, you missed 60% of the auctions you could have entered.

**The two reasons you miss auctions:**

| Metric | Means |
|---|---|
| **Search Lost IS (Budget)** | Your budget ran out |
| **Search Lost IS (Rank)** | Your Ad Rank was too low |

**These three numbers always add to roughly 100%:**

```
Impression Share + Lost IS (Budget) + Lost IS (Rank) ≈ 100%
```

**Think of a shop with limited opening hours and a small doorway.** Lost IS (Budget) is the hours you were closed — customers came, you were not there. Lost IS (Rank) is the customers who walked past because your shop front was less appealing than the one next door. Two completely different problems, needing two completely different fixes.

## The Scaling Decision Framework

This is the core of the lesson. Find your situation in the table.

| Lost IS (Budget) | Lost IS (Rank) | Performance | What it means | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **High (>15%)** | Low | Hitting target | Budget-limited, profitable | ✅ **Raise budget** |
| **High (>15%)** | Low | Above target | Budget-limited, unprofitable | ⚠️ Fix efficiency first |
| Low | **High (>40%)** | Hitting target | Quality or bid limited | ✅ Fix Quality Score / loosen target |
| Low | **High (>40%)** | Above target | Losing auctions you cannot afford | ⚠️ Fix offer, page, or accept the ceiling |
| Low | Low | Hitting target | **Near saturation** | ✅ Expand to new channels or markets |
| Moderate | Moderate | Hitting target | Room in both directions | ✅ Test budget first, then rank |

### The single most important rule

> **Never raise the budget when Lost IS (Budget) is low.**

If you are only losing 3% of impressions to budget, there is almost nothing more to buy. Extra budget will be spent on progressively worse traffic to fill it (Lesson 5.2).

This is the most common scaling mistake. A client says "double the budget" and the account obediently doubles the spend and buys 30% more conversions at twice the cost.

---

## Situation 1: Budget-Limited and Profitable

**Lost IS (Budget) above 15%, performance at or better than target.**

This is the best situation in Google Ads. You are leaving money on the table and you know exactly how to collect it.

### How to scale correctly

1. **Raise the budget by 20–30%.** No more (Lesson 5.1).
2. **Wait 7–14 days.** Smart Bidding needs to adjust.
3. **Check cost per conversion.** Has it held?
4. If yes, repeat. If it has risen more than 15%, you are approaching the ceiling.

### Calculating the opportunity in money

```
Current: 100 conversions/month, PKR 2,000 each, Lost IS (Budget) = 30%

If you captured all of it:
Potential impressions = current ÷ 0.70 = 143% of current
Estimated extra conversions ≈ 43

Extra profit = 43 × (PKR 3,000 value − PKR 2,000 cost) = PKR 43,000/month
```

⚠️ **This is an estimate, not a promise.** The extra impressions will convert somewhat worse than your current ones, because you are already winning the best auctions. Assume 70–85% of the naive estimate.

**Present it as a range to clients**, not a single number.

---

## Situation 2: Rank-Limited

**Lost IS (Rank) above 40%.**

You are losing auctions because your Ad Rank is too low (Lesson 1.1). Money alone will not fix this.

### The three levers, in order of value

**1. Quality Score (best long-term answer)**
Fix Ad Relevance, Expected CTR and Landing Page Experience (Lesson 4.5). Higher quality raises Ad Rank *and* lowers your cost per click. Both at once.

**2. Assets (fastest answer)**
Sitelinks, callouts, images (Lesson 4.2). Asset impact is a direct component of Ad Rank. This can be done in 90 minutes.

**3. Bid target (most immediate, but costs money)**
Loosen your tCPA or lower your tROAS target. This raises bids and wins more auctions — at a higher cost per conversion.

### The order matters

Always try quality and assets first. They raise Ad Rank **for free**. Loosening the target raises Ad Rank **by paying more.**

Most accounts loosen the target first because it takes ten seconds, then wonder why costs rose.

---

## Situation 3: Near Saturation

**Both Lost IS numbers low, and performance is good.**

You are winning most of the available auctions. There is little left to buy in this campaign.

**This is success, and it changes the question.** Growth now has to come from somewhere else:

1. **New keywords and intent clusters** (Lesson 3.5)
2. **New match types** — broad match expansion (Lesson 3.3)
3. **New channels** — Shopping, PMax, Demand Gen (Lesson 1.2)
4. **New geographies** (Lesson 9.3)
5. **New products or services**
6. **Improve conversion rate** so the same traffic produces more (Lesson 0.5)

⚠️ **Do not keep pushing budget into a saturated campaign.** You will buy worse and worse traffic while telling yourself you are scaling.

## Absolute Top IS: The Position Question

**Search absolute top impression share** is how often you appeared in the very first position.

### When position 1 matters

- **Brand terms.** You should own your own name. Target 90%+ absolute top IS.
- **Emergency and urgent searches.** People click the first result and call.
- **Mobile-heavy traffic.** Fewer results are visible on a small screen.

### When it does not

- Considered purchases where people compare several options
- High-value B2B, where buyers research thoroughly
- Any case where position 1 costs a lot more for a similar conversion rate

**How to test it:** compare conversion rate by position using the "Top vs Other" segment. If position 1 converts no better than position 3, paying for position 1 is waste.

---

## Click Share: The Underused Metric

**Click share** is the clicks you received divided by the clicks you were eligible to receive.

**Why it matters more than impression share in some cases:** you can have high impression share and low click share, which means you are appearing but not being chosen. That is an ad copy or offer problem (Lessons 4.1 and 0.2), not a bidding problem.

**The diagnostic:**

| Impression share | Click share | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| High | High | Healthy |
| High | **Low** | You are seen but not chosen — fix the ad and the offer |
| Low | — | Fix impression share first |

---

## The Monthly Scaling Review

Run this once a month, per campaign.

**Step 1.** Export campaigns with: cost, conversions, cost/conv, IS, Lost IS (budget), Lost IS (rank).

**Step 2.** For each campaign, find its situation in the framework table.

**Step 3.** Write the action for each.

**Step 4.** Calculate the money opportunity for every budget-limited, profitable campaign.

**Step 5.** Rank the opportunities by value.

**Step 6.** Act on the top two or three. Not all of them at once — you need to be able to attribute the results.

## 📖 Real-World Example: The Budget Increase That Should Not Have Happened

Bilal's client wanted to grow. Revenue targets had increased and the instruction was simple: *"Double the ad budget."*

Monthly spend: $18,000. The client wanted $36,000.

Before agreeing, Bilal pulled the impression share data.

| Campaign | Spend | Conv | CPA | IS | Lost IS (Budget) | Lost IS (Rank) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brand | $1,100 | 84 | $13 | 91% | 2% | 7% |
| Search - Core | $6,200 | 118 | $53 | 71% | **24%** | 5% |
| Search - Secondary | $4,300 | 51 | $84 | 38% | 4% | **58%** |
| Shopping | $3,900 | 77 | $51 | 62% | **19%** | 19% |
| PMax | $2,500 | 39 | $64 | — | — | — |

**Doubling every budget would have been badly wrong.** Here is what the data said.

### Campaign by campaign

**Brand: near saturation.** 91% IS, 2% lost to budget. Extra money would buy almost nothing. **Action: no change.**

**Search - Core: budget-limited and profitable.** 24% lost to budget, CPA of $53 against a $70 target. **Action: raise budget.** This was the real opportunity.

**Search - Secondary: rank-limited and unprofitable.** 58% lost to rank, CPA of $84 against a $70 target. Raising the budget here would buy nothing, because the problem was Ad Rank, not money. And loosening the target would make an already unprofitable campaign worse. **Action: fix Quality Score.**

**Shopping: budget-limited and profitable.** 19% lost to budget, CPA of $51. **Action: raise budget.**

**PMax: no impression share data available.** Judged on incrementality instead (Lesson 6.3). **Action: hold and measure.**

### What he actually did

Instead of doubling everything, he made three targeted moves.

**Move 1: Raised Search - Core from $6,200 to $8,100** (+30%). Waited 14 days. CPA held at $56. Raised again to $10,500. CPA rose to $61 — still under target. Raised once more to $12,600. CPA hit $68. **Stopped there.**

**Move 2: Raised Shopping from $3,900 to $5,100**, then to $6,600 over six weeks. CPA moved from $51 to $58.

**Move 3: Fixed Search - Secondary rather than funding it.** Its Quality Score averaged 4.1. He rebuilt the ad groups into intent clusters, wrote new RSAs, built the full asset set, and created three dedicated landing pages.

After eight weeks, Quality Score averaged 7.4, Lost IS (Rank) fell from 58% to 21%, and CPA dropped from $84 to $49 — **at the same budget.** Only then did he begin raising it.

### Results at 16 weeks

| | Start | End |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly spend | $18,000 | **$29,400** |
| Conversions | 369 | **683** |
| Cost per conversion | $48.78 | **$43.04** |

**Spend rose 63%, not 100%. Conversions rose 85%. And cost per conversion fell.**

### What doubling everything would have done

Bilal modelled it afterwards, using the actual response curves he had measured:

| | Targeted approach | Naive doubling (estimated) |
|---|---|---|
| Spend | $29,400 | $36,000 |
| Conversions | 683 | ~520 |
| Cost per conversion | $43.04 | ~$69 |

**The naive approach would have spent $6,600 more per month for roughly 163 fewer conversions.**

Most of the wasted money would have gone into Brand (nothing left to buy) and Search - Secondary (a rank problem that money cannot solve).

**The client's reaction:** *"So the answer to 'can we grow' was yes — but not the way I asked."*

## 📌 Key Takeaways

- Lost IS (Budget) and Lost IS (Rank) are completely different problems with completely different fixes.
- Never raise the budget when Lost IS (Budget) is low. There is nothing left to buy.
- For rank problems, fix Quality Score and assets first — they raise Ad Rank for free. Loosen the target last.
- Scale in 20–30% steps, waiting 7–14 days between each, and stop when cost per conversion rises past target.
- Low loss in both directions means saturation. Growth must then come from new keywords, channels or markets.

## 🎯 Expert Challenge

Build a scaling model with response curves.

- For your main campaign, gather historical data at different budget levels and plot the relationship between spend and conversions. This curve is almost always concave — each extra pound buys slightly less.
- Find the point where marginal cost per conversion exceeds your maximum acceptable CAC (Lesson 0.3). That is your true budget ceiling, and almost nobody knows theirs.
- Build the same analysis for rank-limited campaigns: model how much Quality Score improvement would be worth in recovered impression share and reduced cost per click.
- Design the scaling programme: the sequence of increases, the waiting periods, the measurement at each step, and the stopping rule.
- Then produce the growth forecast the client actually needs: given their revenue target, what spend is needed, through which campaigns, and what the resulting cost per conversion will be. Include the honest statement of where the ceiling is, because a forecast without a ceiling is a fantasy.
