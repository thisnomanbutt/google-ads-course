# Lesson 9.6 — Bidding on Competitor Names: Is It Worth It?

*Phase 5 · Running Big Accounts and Clients — Module 9 · Managing Large, Complex Accounts*

## Quick Recap From Lesson 1.5

You know the trademark rules: you can generally bid on a competitor's name, but you usually cannot use it in your ad text. Now we look at whether you should, and how to defend against it.

## The Honest Assessment First

Conquesting is often taught as a clever tactic. Here is the balanced view.

### The case for it

- The searcher is in-market **right now**
- You appear at the exact moment they are considering a competitor
- If your offer is really better, this is your best chance to say so
- Competitor brand terms are sometimes cheap, because only a few advertisers bid on them

### The case against it

- **Quality Score will be low** — typically 2–4 (Lesson 4.5). Your ad and page cannot be relevant to someone else's brand name.
- **Low Quality Score means a high cost per click.**
- **Conversion rates are usually poor.** Someone searching a specific brand often wants that brand.
- **It invites retaliation.** They will bid on your name, and both of you will pay more for traffic you were both getting free.
- Some markets restrict it more tightly than others.

### The honest verdict

> **Conquesting works when you have a genuine, demonstrable advantage over the competitor being targeted. It fails when you are simply hoping to steal traffic.**

**Do it if:**
- Your offer is meaningfully better on a dimension the customer cares about
- The competitor has a known weakness (poor reviews, price, service, availability)
- You have the budget to sustain it and absorb retaliation
- Your landing page directly addresses the comparison

**Do not do it if:**
- Your offer is similar or worse
- Your budget is tight — spend it on higher-intent traffic first
- You cannot handle a bidding war on your own brand
- You have not yet maximised your own high-intent traffic (Lesson 8.1)

⚠️ **That last point matters most.** If you are only capturing 50% of the impressions on your own high-intent keywords, buy those first. They are cheaper and convert far better.

## Building a Conquesting Campaign

If you proceed, build it properly. A badly built conquesting campaign is expensive.

### Rule 1: Always a separate campaign

Never mix conquesting keywords into your normal campaigns. They have completely different economics and will drag down every average.

```
Search | PK-KHI | Conquesting | Exact | Sales
```

Separate budget, separate targets, separate reporting.

### Rule 2: Exact match only

Broad match on a competitor's name will match a vast range of loosely related searches at terrible cost.

Use exact match on the specific brand terms you want:

```
[competitor name]
[competitor name reviews]
[competitor name price]
[competitor name alternative]
[competitor name vs]
```

### Rule 3: Target the comparison intent, not the brand alone

The highest-value conquesting keywords are not the brand name alone — they are the **comparison and alternative** searches.

| Keyword type | Intent | Value |
|---|---|---|
| `[competitor]` | Wants that company. Often navigational. | 🔴 Low |
| `[competitor] reviews` | Evaluating. Open to doubt. | 🟡 Medium |
| `[competitor] price` | Cost-sensitive. Open to alternatives. | 🟢 Good |
| `[competitor] alternative` | **Actively looking to switch** | 🟢🟢 Best |
| `[competitor] vs [you]` | Already comparing you | 🟢🟢 Best |
| `[competitor] complaints` | Unhappy customer | 🟢🟢 Best |

**Start with the bottom four.** They are cheaper, convert far better, and provoke less retaliation.

### Rule 4: You cannot use their name in the ad

From Lesson 1.5. Your ad text must not contain their trademark unless you are an authorised reseller or a genuine comparison site.

**So what do you say?**

Write ads about the **thing they lack**:

❌ "Better Than [Competitor]" — trademark violation
✅ "Water Filters That Work Without Electricity"
✅ "30-Day Returns. Most Brands Give 7."
✅ "Free Installation Included"

**Lead with your differentiator** (Lesson 0.2). The searcher will make the comparison themselves.

### Rule 5: A dedicated comparison landing page

Send this traffic to a page built for comparison — not your homepage.

**What the page needs:**
- A clear, factual comparison table
- Honest treatment (say where the competitor is better; it builds credibility)
- Your differentiator prominent
- Strong risk removal — switching is a risk, so remove it
- Social proof from people who switched

⚠️ **Be truthful in comparisons.** False claims about a competitor create legal risk beyond Google's policies, and Google's misrepresentation policy applies too (Lesson 1.5).

### Rule 6: A tight budget and a tight target

Cap it. Conquesting should be a small percentage of your account — typically 5–10% — until it proves itself.

Set a tighter tCPA than your main campaigns, because you expect worse conversion rates and want to limit exposure.

## Defending Your Own Brand

The other half of this lesson, and the more important half for most businesses.

### How to know if you are being attacked

**Method 1: Auction Insights on your brand campaign.**
Any competitor appearing there is bidding on your brand terms.

**Method 2: Search your own name** in an incognito window, with your location set correctly. Who appears above you?

**Method 3: Watch your brand CPC over time.** Rising brand cost per click with stable volume usually means new competitors have entered.

### The defence

**1. Run a brand campaign.** From Lesson 1.2. This is the primary defence.

**2. Target high absolute top impression share.** Aim for 90%+ on your own brand. You should own position 1 for your own name.

**3. Keep brand Quality Score high.** It should be 9–10. Your ad and page are really the most relevant result for your own name, so your cost per click should be very low — usually a fraction of what a competitor pays to appear there.

**This asymmetry is your main advantage.** They pay a lot to appear on your brand. You pay very little to defend it.

**4. Use every asset** (Lesson 4.2). A brand ad with eight sitelinks, callouts, images and a price table occupies a large amount of screen. A competitor's plain text ad above it is easy to scroll past.

**5. Make the brand ad really useful.** Sitelinks to login, support, delivery information, and specific products. Give existing customers what they came for.

### Should you escalate?

If a competitor bids on your brand, you can retaliate by bidding on theirs.

**The honest analysis:**

| Outcome | Likelihood |
|---|---|
| They stop, you both save money | Sometimes, if they are rational |
| Both of you spend more, neither gains | **Most common** |
| You gain more than you lose | Possible if their brand is bigger than yours |
| You lose more than you gain | Possible if your brand is bigger than theirs |

**The key asymmetry:** the advertiser with the **stronger brand** loses more in a mutual conquesting war, because they have more valuable brand traffic to defend.

**If you have the stronger brand, escalation is usually against your interest.** Defend well and ignore them.

**If you are the smaller brand, conquesting is more attractive** — you have less to lose and more to gain.

⚠️ **Never escalate emotionally.** Model the economics first. Bidding wars are usually a transfer of money from both advertisers to Google.

## Auction Insights: Reading It Properly

Auction Insights is really useful and widely misread.

**Where:** campaign or ad group → Insights → Auction insights.

### The metrics

| Metric | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Impression share** | How often they appeared, out of eligible auctions |
| **Overlap rate** | How often you both appeared in the same auction |
| **Position above rate** | When you both appeared, how often they were above you |
| **Top of page rate** | How often their ad appeared above the organic results |
| **Absolute top of page rate** | How often they were in position 1 |
| **Outranking share** | How often you ranked above them, or showed when they did not |

### How to read it

**Pattern 1: New domain appears with rising share**
A new competitor has entered. Watch their trajectory over three months. A well-funded new entrant can change your market's economics (Lesson 8.4).

**Pattern 2: High overlap rate, low position above rate**
You are consistently beating them. Good position — do not overspend to improve it further.

**Pattern 3: High overlap rate, high position above rate**
They are consistently beating you. Investigate: is it their bid, or their Quality Score? Check your own Quality Score first (Lesson 4.5) — fixing it is cheaper than outbidding them.

**Pattern 4: Low overlap rate, high impression share for them**
They are in the market but competing for different searches than you. Investigate which — there may be a segment you are missing.

**Pattern 5: Your outranking share falling over time**
You are losing ground. Combine with impression share data (Lesson 8.1) to diagnose whether it is budget or rank.

### The limitation

⚠️ **Auction Insights shows domains, not strategy.** It tells you who competes and how often. It does not tell you their budget, their targets, their offer or their profitability.

**Do not infer strategy from Auction Insights alone.** Combine it with manual SERP review (Lesson 3.2) and offer teardown (Lesson 0.2).

## 📖 Real-World Example: The Conquesting Campaign That Worked, and the One That Did Not

Bilal ran conquesting for two clients in the same quarter. One succeeded, one failed. The difference explains the whole lesson.

### Client A: The failure

A UK office supplies retailer wanted to conquest a large, well-known national competitor.

**The setup:**
- Exact match on the competitor's brand name and 12 variations
- Budget: £4,000/month
- Landing page: their homepage

**Results after 8 weeks:**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Spend | £7,840 |
| Quality Score | 2 |
| Average CPC | £6.20 |
| Clicks | 1,264 |
| Conversions | 11 |
| Cost per conversion | **£712** |
| Their normal cost per conversion | £48 |

**Fifteen times their normal cost.**

**And it got worse.** Within six weeks, the competitor began bidding on their brand name. Their own brand cost per click rose from £0.34 to £1.15 — a 238% increase on their cheapest and most valuable traffic.

**The net effect:** they spent £7,840 on conquesting to gain 11 conversions, and their brand defence costs rose by roughly £1,400 a month permanently.

**Why it failed:**
1. **No genuine advantage.** Their offer was broadly similar. There was no reason for a searcher to switch.
2. **Homepage landing page.** No comparison, no reason given.
3. **They targeted the brand name alone**, which is largely navigational intent — people who wanted that company.
4. **They were the smaller brand**, but the competitor's retaliation still hurt because their brand traffic was their most efficient channel.

They paused it after 8 weeks. The competitor kept bidding on their brand for another five months.

### Client B: The success

A specialist B2B software company wanted to conquest a large incumbent competitor.

**The genuine advantage:** the incumbent needed an annual contract with a lengthy setup. The client offered monthly billing with same-week setup. This was a real, documented difference that mattered to a specific segment.

**The setup:**
- **Not** the brand name alone. Only comparison intent:
  ```
  [competitor] alternative
  [competitor] vs
  [competitor] pricing
  [competitor] contract length
  [competitor] reviews
  [competitor] cancel
  ```
- Budget: $2,000/month, capped
- **A dedicated comparison page**, with an honest table that stated clearly where the competitor was better (more integrations, larger support team)
- Ad copy leading with "No Annual Contract. Live in 5 Days."
- Separate campaign, tighter tCPA than their main campaigns

**Results after 12 weeks:**

| Metric | Conquesting campaign | Their normal campaigns |
|---|---|---|
| Spend | $5,900 | — |
| Quality Score | 3 | 8 |
| Average CPC | $9.40 | $3.80 |
| Conversions (customers) | 41 | — |
| **Cost per customer** | **$144** | **$112** |

**Cost per customer was 29% higher than their normal channels — and they kept it running.**

**Why?** Because $144 was still well within their acceptable range (their LTV-based ceiling was $200), and these were customers they would never otherwise have reached. The traffic was really incremental.

**And the retaliation?** The incumbent did not retaliate. Bilal's theory: the client was small enough that the incumbent's team either did not notice or did not consider them worth responding to.

**That is a real strategic factor.** Conquesting a much larger competitor sometimes provokes no response, because you are beneath their attention.

### The comparison

| | Client A (failed) | Client B (succeeded) |
|---|---|---|
| Genuine advantage? | ❌ No | ✅ Yes, documented |
| Keywords | Brand name alone | Comparison intent only |
| Landing page | Homepage | Dedicated comparison page |
| Honesty about competitor | N/A | ✅ Stated where they were better |
| Budget control | Uncapped growth | Capped at $2,000 |
| Retaliation | ✅ Yes, costly | ❌ None |
| Result | £712/conversion | $144/customer |

**The difference was not tactical execution. It was whether they had something really better to say.**

## 📌 Key Takeaways

- Conquesting works only when you have a genuine, demonstrable advantage. Otherwise it is expensive traffic theft that fails.
- Target comparison intent — alternative, vs, pricing, reviews — not the brand name alone.
- You generally cannot use a competitor's trademark in your ad text. Lead with your differentiator instead.
- Defend your own brand with a dedicated campaign, 90%+ absolute top impression share, and a full asset set.
- The stronger brand loses more in a mutual bidding war. Model the economics before escalating.

## 🎯 Expert Challenge

Build a complete competitive strategy, offensive and defensive.

- Build the competitor matrix: for each competitor, their offer, their pricing, their weaknesses, and whether you have a documented advantage. Only competitors where you can answer the last column are conquesting candidates.
- Model the escalation economics: if you conquest competitor X and they retaliate on your brand, what is the net cost? Include your brand traffic volume, current brand CPC, and the likely increase.
- Design the defensive structure: brand campaign structure, target impression share, asset build, and the alert that tells you when a new competitor enters your brand auctions.
- Build the comparison content assets: an honest comparison page per competitor, with the discipline of stating where they are better. Credibility is what converts a comparison visitor.
- Design the measurement: conquesting must be judged on incremental customers at an acceptable cost, in its own campaign, never blended into account averages.
- Finally, write the decision rule for escalation: the specific conditions under which you would retaliate against a competitor bidding on your brand, and the conditions under which you would on purpose not. Deciding this in advance prevents an emotional, expensive response later.
