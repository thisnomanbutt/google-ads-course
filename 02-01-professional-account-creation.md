## Two Choices You Can Never Change

When you create a Google Ads account, you choose:

1. **Currency**
2. **Timezone**

**These cannot be changed. Ever.** Not by you, not by support, not by a Google rep.

If you get them wrong, your only option is to build a completely new account and lose all your history, all your conversion data and all your Smart Bidding learning.

**Think of pouring a concrete foundation for a house.** You can change the paint, the furniture, the roof. You cannot move the foundation once it sets. Take the extra ten minutes now.

---

## Choosing the Currency

### The rule

**Use the currency the business actually operates in and reports profit in.**

### Why it matters

- Your bids, budgets and conversion values are all in this currency
- Google converts your ad costs into this currency for billing
- Your reporting and your client's accounting must match
- Currency conversion happens at Google's monthly rate, adding a small cost and some unpredictability

### Practical guidance

| Situation | Currency to choose |
|---|---|
| Pakistani business, Pakistani customers | PKR |
| Pakistani agency, US client selling in USD | **USD** (the client's currency) |
| Business sells in multiple countries | The currency they report profit in, usually their home currency |
| You are unsure | Ask: "In which currency does the owner know their profit?" |

⚠️ **Common mistake:** a Pakistani freelancer creates a US client's account in PKR because it feels familiar. Now every report needs conversion, the client cannot match it to their books, and the numbers drift with the exchange rate. Always match the business.

---

## Choosing the Timezone

### The rule

**Use the timezone of the business's main market — where the customers are.**

### Why it matters more than people think

- Your **daily budget resets at midnight in this timezone**. If the timezone is wrong, your budget refreshes in the middle of your customers' shopping day.
- Ad schedules run on this clock
- All reporting dates use this clock
- "Yesterday" means something different if you and the account disagree about midnight

### Practical guidance

| Situation | Timezone |
|---|---|
| Business and customers in Pakistan | (GMT+05:00) Pakistan Standard Time |
| Pakistani agency, client's customers in New York | Eastern Time |
| Client's customers across the whole US | Usually the client's head office timezone, or Central as a compromise |
| Global business | The timezone of the largest market |

**Never choose a timezone for your own convenience.** You will adjust. The budget cannot.

## The Third Permanent Choice: Account Type

When you sign up, Google pushes you hard toward **Smart Mode** (sometimes called Smart Campaigns). It is designed for small business owners with no experience.

**You do not want it.** Smart Mode gives you:
- Almost no control over keywords
- No proper bid strategies
- No campaign types beyond the basics
- Very limited reporting

### How to get the real thing (Expert Mode)

The signup flow changes over time, but the principle stays the same. Look for one of these:

- A link saying **"Switch to Expert Mode"** near the bottom of the page
- **"Create an account without a campaign"**
- Skipping the campaign creation step entirely

**If you cannot find it:** complete the signup, then look in Settings for the option to switch. You can convert from Smart Mode to Expert Mode. You cannot easily go back — and you would not want to.

**Do not create a campaign during signup.** Google will pressure you to. Create the account first, set everything up properly, then build your campaign on purpose.

## Billing Setup: The Practical Details

### Payment settings you choose

**Automatic payments** — you spend, then Google charges you. Standard for most accounts.

**Manual payments (prepay)** — you add money first, then spend it. Available in some countries, and really useful when:
- Cards are unreliable
- You want a hard spending ceiling
- The client wants to control exposure

### The billing structure that protects everyone

From Lesson 0.7, remember the rule: **never fund a client's ad spend with your own money.**

| Setup | Who pays Google | Recommended? |
|---|---|---|
| Client's card on client's account | Client | ✅ Best. Always try this first |
| Client prepays you, you pay Google | Client, in advance | ✅ Acceptable |
| Your card, invoice the client later | **You** | ❌ Never |
| Monthly invoicing from Google | Client, on credit | ✅ Good for larger accounts, needs approval |

### Billing details must match reality

- The business name on billing should match the real registered business
- The address should be the real business address
- Mismatches trigger verification problems and, in some cases, suspension

### For Pakistani cards

From Lesson 0.7:
1. Call your bank and enable **international AND recurring** online transactions
2. Ask about the monthly foreign transaction limit
3. Expect the first charge to sometimes fail — this is common, not a disaster
4. Keep a second payment method on the account as a backup

⚠️ **Add a backup payment method on every account.** A failed card means your ads stop, usually at the worst possible moment. A backup card costs nothing and prevents a bad Monday.

## Advertiser Verification

Google needs businesses to check their identity. This has become stricter every year.

**What you typically need:**
- Business registration documents
- Proof of address
- Identity documents for the account owner
- Sometimes, proof of the relationship between the payment method and the business

**Why to do it early:**
- Unverified accounts can face restrictions
- The process can take days or weeks
- Discovering this during a campaign launch is painful

**Do it on day one, before you build anything.**

## The Complete New Account Checklist

Work through this in order. Do not skip.

**Before you click anything:**
- [ ] 1. Confirm the currency with the business owner in writing
- [ ] 2. Confirm the timezone based on where customers are
- [ ] 3. Confirm who owns the account (the client — see Lesson 1.4)

**During creation:**
- [ ] 4. Switch to Expert Mode
- [ ] 5. Create the account **without** a campaign
- [ ] 6. Set currency and timezone carefully. Read them twice before confirming.

**Immediately after creation:**
- [ ] 7. Set up billing with the correct payer, plus a backup method
- [ ] 8. Start advertiser verification
- [ ] 9. **Turn off auto-apply recommendations** (Lesson 1.3)
- [ ] 10. Turn off automatically created assets until you can review them
- [ ] 11. Link the account to your MCC (if you use one)
- [ ] 12. Set user access properly — minimum access needed per person
- [ ] 13. Set up conversion tracking **before** any campaign goes live (Lesson 2.3)
- [ ] 14. Build your custom columns and saved views (Lesson 1.3)

**Only then:** build your first campaign.

## Multiple Accounts: When Is It Allowed?

Google's rule is that you should not run multiple accounts for the same business promoting the same content. Doing so can trigger a Circumvention suspension (Lesson 1.5).

**Legitimate reasons for separate accounts:**
- Really separate legal businesses
- Different countries with different currencies (this is the most common valid reason)
- A separate brand with its own website and its own business identity

**Not legitimate:**
- "I want to test two strategies"  → use experiments instead (Lesson 13.1)
- "I want a backup in case one is suspended" → this *is* circumvention
- "One account for brand, one for non-brand" → use campaigns

If you really need multiple accounts, keep them all under one MCC, with transparent, real business details. Hidden duplicates are what Google punishes.

## 📖 Real-World Example: The PKR Account That Cost a Client

Bilal's second US client was a home services company in Texas. He set up the account quickly on a Sunday evening, using habits from his Pakistani accounts.

He chose **PKR** as the currency and **Pakistan Standard Time** as the timezone.

At first, nothing seemed wrong. The ads ran. Leads came in.

Then the problems arrived, one by one.

**Problem 1: Budget resets at the wrong time.** The daily budget refreshed at midnight Pakistan time — which is 2pm in Texas. So every day, the budget was often exhausted before the American evening, which was the client's best lead time. He was always missing his best hours and could not work out why.

**Problem 2: Reporting arguments.** The client's monthly report showed "PKR 1,240,000 spent". The client's bank statement showed "$4,380". These two numbers never matched exactly, because Google converted at its own monthly rate. Every month brought a confused email.

**Problem 3: Broken profit maths.** The client's product prices were in USD. The conversion values arriving in Google were being converted. His breakeven ROAS calculation (Lesson 0.3) drifted every month with the exchange rate. He could not set a reliable tROAS target.

**Problem 4: Reporting dates.** "Yesterday" in the account meant a different 24 hours than "yesterday" for the client. Comparing to their CRM data needed manual adjustment every single time.

**The fix.** There was no fix. He had to build a completely new account in USD and Central Time.

**What that cost:**
- 4 months of conversion history: gone
- Smart Bidding learning: reset to zero
- 3 weeks of worse performance while the new account learned
- Roughly $2,100 of extra cost during the relearning period
- A very uncomfortable conversation with the client

**Ten minutes of thought on day one would have prevented all of it.**

Bilal now has one rule written at the top of his onboarding document: *"Before creating any account, write the currency and timezone in the client email and get a written 'yes'."*

## 📌 Key Takeaways

- Currency and timezone can never be changed. Match the business, not your convenience.
- The daily budget resets at midnight in the account's timezone. Get this right or lose your best hours.
- Always create the account in Expert Mode, and without a campaign.
- The client's payment method belongs on the client's account. Never fund ad spend yourself.
- Add a backup payment method and start verification on day one.

## 💪 Practical Exercise (20 minutes)

Audit every account you have access to.

1. For each account, write down the currency and the timezone.
2. Write down where the customers actually are.
3. Mark any account where these do not match. Note what problems it is causing.
4. Check whether a backup payment method exists on each account.
5. Check whether auto-apply is off on each account.
6. Write a one-page onboarding checklist you will use for the next account you create.
