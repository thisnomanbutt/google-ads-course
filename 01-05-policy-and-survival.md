## Why This Lesson Is Worth More Than It Looks

A suspension can end a client relationship in one day. It can end a business.

And here is the painful part: **most suspensions happen to honest people who did nothing wrong on purpose.** They just did not know a rule.

**Think of driving in a new city.** You are a careful driver. But you did not know that one street is one-way. The fine is the same as for a reckless driver. Learning the rules first is far cheaper than learning them from a penalty.

## The Suspension Types, Ranked by Frequency

### 1. Misrepresentation — the most common by far

Google's definition is broad. In practice it means: **the user cannot easily understand who you are, what you are selling, and what will happen if they buy.**

Common triggers:

- **Missing business information.** No address, no phone number, no company name on the site.
- **No clear pricing.** Or a price that changes between the ad and the checkout.
- **Missing refund, return or cancellation policy.**
- **Unrealistic promises.** "Guaranteed #1 ranking", "Cure diabetes", "Earn $10,000/month from home".
- **Fake urgency.** A countdown timer that resets every time you reload the page.
- **Unclear subscription terms.** A "free trial" that silently starts charging.
- **A landing page that does not match the ad.** (This is offer continuity from Lesson 0.5 — now a policy issue too.)

### 2. Circumvention of Systems — the most dangerous

This is Google's version of a red card. It means: you tried to get around Google's rules or its review systems.

Triggers include:

- **Opening a new account after a suspension.** This is the big one.
- Running ads for a business or site that has already been banned
- **Cloaking** — showing Google's review system one page and real users a different page
- Using redirects to hide the real destination
- Using multiple accounts to serve ads for the same suspended business
- Manipulating the review process in any way

⚠️ **Understand this clearly.** Circumvention suspensions are extremely hard to reverse, and Google can link your new accounts to your old ones through payment methods, IP addresses, business details and website domains.

**If your account is suspended, do NOT create a new one.** That single reaction turns a fixable problem into a permanent one. Appeal instead. This is the most important sentence in this lesson.

### 3. Destination what you need

The page you send people to must work properly:

- It must load
- It must work on mobile
- It must not need unusual software
- It must not contain malware
- It must not be under construction
- It must not open pop-ups that trap the user

### 4. Restricted content and industries

Some categories are allowed but need certification, licences or extra checks. These vary by country:

- Healthcare and medicines
- Financial services, loans and investments
- Gambling
- Alcohol
- Legal services in some markets
- Cryptocurrency

**Rule:** if your client is in a restricted industry, check the certification what you need *before* you build the account, not after.

### 5. Compromised site

Your website was hacked and Google detected malware or spam. Fix the site, then appeal. This one is usually straightforward once the site is clean.

## Trademarks: What You Can and Cannot Do

This confuses everybody. Here is the clear version.

### Bidding on a competitor's brand name as a keyword

**Generally allowed** in most countries, including the US, UK and Pakistan. You can bid on "Competitor Name" as a keyword.

**But be careful:** rules differ by region and some markets restrict it more tightly. And it is often a poor strategy anyway — low Quality Score, high cost, defensive retaliation. We cover the strategy in Lesson 9.6.

### Using a competitor's trademark in your ad text

**Generally not allowed.** You cannot write "Better than Competitor Name" in your headline if that name is trademarked and you are not authorised.

**Exceptions usually include:**
- Authorised resellers who really sell the product
- Informational sites that really review or compare products
- The trademark owner themselves

### If someone complains about your ads

Google will investigate and may restrict your ads. You can respond with evidence of your right to use the term.

### If a competitor uses YOUR trademark

You can file a trademark complaint with Google. You need to be the trademark owner or an authorised representative, and you need registration details.

⚠️ **Important:** Google enforces its trademark policy. It does not decide trademark law. A Google decision is not a legal ruling, and a legal dispute is a matter for lawyers, not for support chat.

## Merchant Center Suspensions (For E-commerce)

Merchant Center has its own rules, separate from Google Ads. A Merchant Center suspension stops your Shopping and PMax product ads immediately.

### The most common causes

**1. Price mismatch.** The price in your feed does not match the price on your website. Even a small difference triggers this. Currency mismatches count too.

**2. Missing or unclear return policy.** You need a clear, findable returns and refunds page.

**3. Missing contact information.** You need at least two contact methods: a real email, a phone number, a physical address, or a contact form.

**4. Missing or incorrect tax and shipping settings.** The total the customer pays must be predictable.

**5. Insufficient checkout process.** The user must be able to actually complete a purchase without creating an account or contacting you.

**6. Misrepresentation.** Same principles as Google Ads.

### The prevention checklist

Run this before launching any Shopping campaign:

- [ ] Feed prices match website prices exactly, including currency
- [ ] Returns policy page exists and is linked in the footer
- [ ] Contact page has email, phone and address
- [ ] Shipping costs and delivery times are stated
- [ ] Terms and conditions page exists
- [ ] Privacy policy page exists
- [ ] Checkout works end to end — test it yourself with a real order
- [ ] Site has a valid SSL certificate (https, with the padlock)
- [ ] Business name is consistent across the site, the feed and the payment page

Nine checkboxes. They prevent the large most of Merchant Center suspensions.

## Prevention: The Pre-Launch Policy Audit

Do this before every new account goes live. It takes 20 minutes and saves weeks.

**On the website:**
1. Business name, address and phone visible in the footer
2. About page with real information about real people
3. Privacy policy, terms, and refund/cancellation policy
4. Pricing visible or clearly explained
5. SSL certificate active
6. Site loads on mobile without pop-up traps

**In the ads:**
7. No absolute claims you cannot prove ("guaranteed", "best", "#1", "cure")
8. No competitor trademarks in text
9. Ad promise matches page content exactly
10. Any special claim has visible proof on the page

**In the account:**
11. Billing details match the real business
12. Business verification completed
13. One business, one account — no duplicates

## The Appeal Process: A Step-by-Step SOP

When suspension happens, panic is the enemy. Work the process.

### Step 1: Read the actual reason (10 minutes)

Do not skim. Find the exact policy name in the notification. "Misrepresentation" and "Circumvention" need completely different responses.

### Step 2: Do NOT create a new account

Say it again. Do not create a new account. Do not use a friend's account. Do not change the domain and try again. All of these turn a fixable suspension into a permanent ban.

### Step 3: Find and fix the real problem (1–3 hours)

Go through the prevention checklist above. Be honest. Look for what a stranger would find confusing or misleading, not what you know to be true.

Fix everything you find, not just the one thing you think caused it. Reviewers look at the whole picture.

### Step 4: Document your fixes

Take screenshots of every change. Note the date and time. You will attach or reference these.

### Step 5: Wait 24 hours before appealing

Give your site time to update, caches to clear, and your own emotions to settle. An angry appeal written at midnight helps nobody.

### Step 6: Submit one clear appeal

Use the template. Submit once.

### Step 7: Wait, then follow up properly

Reviews commonly take a few days. If you get rejected, **do not immediately resubmit the identical appeal**. Find something more to fix, then appeal again with new information.

### Step 8: If you exhaust appeals

Use the Google Ads Community forums. Product Experts there sometimes have escalation paths. Post with full detail and no emotion.

## The Appeal Letter Formula

Every successful appeal has the same five parts:

1. **Acknowledge** the policy without arguing
2. **Explain** what caused it, factually and briefly
3. **List** exactly what you changed
4. **Show** proof (URLs, screenshots)
5. **Commit** to compliance going forward

**What kills appeals:**
- Arguing that the policy is unfair
- Emotional pressure ("my family depends on this")
- Blaming Google
- Vague statements like "we have fixed everything"
- Repeating the same appeal word for word

**What wins appeals:** specific, verifiable, humble, complete.

The full template is in `templates/appeal-letter-template.md`. Copy it, fill in the blanks, and keep a filled-in copy for every account you manage.

## 📖 Real-World Example: Six Days to Recovery

Bilal's US supplement client was suspended on a Thursday for **Misrepresentation**. Revenue stopped that day. The client was furious.

**What Bilal did NOT do:** he did not create a new account, and he did not send an angry message to support that night.

**What he actually did:**

**Thursday evening.** He read the notification carefully. Policy: Misrepresentation. He wrote down the exact wording.

**Friday morning (3 hours).** He audited the site as a stranger would. He found four problems, not one:

1. The refund policy page existed but was not linked anywhere a customer could find it
2. Three product pages said "clinically proven" with no study cited
3. The "contact us" page had only a form — no phone, no address
4. A countdown timer on the offer page reset on every page reload

He had built none of these. The client's web developer had. That did not matter.

**Friday afternoon.** He fixed all four:
- Refund policy linked in the footer on every page
- Removed "clinically proven" from all pages and all ads, replacing it with the actual, honest claim
- Added a real US phone number, business address and support email
- Removed the fake countdown timer entirely

He screenshotted every change with timestamps.

**Saturday.** He waited. On purpose.

**Sunday.** He submitted one appeal using the template. Specific. Calm. Complete. He listed all four fixes with URLs.

**Wednesday.** Account reinstated. Six days from suspension to live.

**What he did next — and this is the professional part.** He sent the client a short document titled "How we prevent this happening again", containing the 13-point pre-launch checklist and a monthly policy review he would now run.

He did not lose the client. He deepened the relationship.

**Compare that to the alternative.** A panicked new account on Friday night would have triggered a Circumvention suspension. That client would likely have lost their entire Google Ads presence permanently.

## 📌 Key Takeaways

- Misrepresentation is the most common suspension. It usually means unclear business information, not lying.
- Never create a new account after a suspension. That is Circumvention, and it is close to permanent.
- You can usually bid on a competitor's name, but you generally cannot use it in your ad text.
- Merchant Center has separate rules. Price mismatch and missing policy pages cause most suspensions.
- Winning appeals are specific, calm, complete and submitted once.

## 🎯 Expert Challenge

Build a complete policy risk system for an agency.

- Create a risk rating for each client: low, medium or high, based on industry, claim style and site quality. High-risk clients get monthly reviews; low-risk get quarterly.
- Write the pre-launch audit as a formal checklist document with sign-off fields for you and the client.
- Prepare a filled-in appeal letter template for each client in advance, with their business details already in place. In a real suspension you will have 20 minutes of clear thinking, not two hours.
- Write the client communication script for day one of a suspension: what you say, what you promise, and what you on purpose do not promise. Never promise reinstatement — you do not control it.
- Finally, write the business continuity plan: what the client does for revenue during a suspension of 3, 10 or 30 days. A client with a plan stays calm. A client without one blames you.
