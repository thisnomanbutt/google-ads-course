## Who Reps Actually Are

Understanding this changes how you work with them.

### The types you may encounter

| Type | Who they are | What they can do |
|---|---|---|
| **Chat/email support** | Outsourced support agents | Billing, basic account questions, simple fixes |
| **Onboarding specialist** | Temporary, assigned to new or growing accounts | Setup guidance, usually for a fixed period |
| **Account strategist / rep** | Assigned to larger accounts | Strategy, betas, escalation, optimisation suggestions |
| **Agency partner manager** | Assigned to agencies in the partner programme | Multi-account support, training, escalation |
| **Product specialist** | Deep expertise in one product | Technical help on PMax, measurement, feeds |

### The honest picture

**What is really valuable:**
- Escalation paths you cannot access otherwise
- Beta access before general release
- Advance notice of product changes
- Technical specialists for complex problems
- Faster policy reviews

**What to be realistic about:**

⚠️ **Reps have targets, and those targets are usually about your spend and about adoption of Google's products.** That does not make them dishonest — most are really trying to help. But their incentives and your client's incentives are not identical.

**A rep suggesting you increase budget by 40% may be right. They may also be measured on your account's growth.** Both can be true simultaneously.

**Your job:** take the value, evaluate every recommendation on its merits, and decline politely when it does not serve your client.

## How to Get a Rep

Reps are assigned based on spend, growth potential and market. There is no application form, but you can improve your visibility.

### Route 1: Spend threshold

Accounts above a certain monthly spend attract attention automatically. The threshold varies by market and changes over time, and Google does not publish it.

**In practice:** meaningful spend in a developed market attracts contact. Smaller spend in a smaller market may not.

### Route 2: Growth trajectory

**Google notices accounts that are growing quickly**, even at modest absolute spend. A account tripling its spend over six months is more interesting than a larger account that is flat.

### Route 3: Consolidate under one MCC

From Lesson 10.1: ten accounts at PKR 200,000 each, scattered across ten logins, are invisible. The same ten accounts under one MCC total PKR 2,000,000 and are visible as a portfolio.

**This is the most practical route for agencies in smaller markets.**

### Route 4: The Google Partner programme

Google has a partner programme for agencies. What you need typically include:

- A spend threshold across your managed accounts
- Certifications held by a proportion of your team
- Performance what you need
- Account activity levels

**Benefits typically include:** a partner manager, training resources, and in some cases event access and promotional support.

⚠️ **Check the current what you need directly.** They change, and they differ by region.

### Route 5: Be really responsive

Reps have limited time and prioritise advertisers who engage. If you ignore three emails and then request help urgently, you are at the back of the queue.

## Running the Relationship Well

### Prepare for every call

Reps typically arrive with a prepared deck of recommendations. If you have no agenda, you will spend the whole call on theirs.

**Bring your own agenda:**

1. **Your questions first.** Specific technical problems, policy questions, product roadmap.
2. **Your asks.** Beta access, escalation on a stuck issue, a specialist introduction.
3. **Then** their recommendations.

**Send your agenda before the call.** It changes the dynamic entirely and reps generally appreciate it.

### Be specific about what you want

❌ "Can you help us improve performance?"
✅ "We are seeing a 40% discrepancy between offline conversion uploads and our CRM records. Can you connect us with a measurement specialist?"

**Specific requests get specific help.** Vague requests get a generic deck.

### Build the relationship over time

- Respond to emails, even briefly
- Attend the occasional call even when you have nothing pressing
- Share results when their suggestion worked — this really helps them and builds goodwill
- Be honest when it did not work, with data

**A rep who trusts your judgement will bring you better opportunities.**

## Evaluating Recommendations

This is the skill. Reps will bring recommendations. Some are excellent; some would harm your client.

### The evaluation framework

For every recommendation, ask:

**1. What is the mechanism?** How specifically would this improve results? If nobody can explain the mechanism, be cautious.

**2. What is the evidence?** Is there data from this account, or is it a general best practice?

**3. What would it cost?** Money, time, and risk.

**4. What is the downside case?** If it goes wrong, how bad is it and how quickly can we reverse it?

**5. Does it move the profit column?** From Lesson 1.3 — not the conversion column, not the optimisation score. The profit column.

### Common recommendations, honestly assessed

| Recommendation | Usually good when | Be cautious when |
|---|---|---|
| **Increase budget** | Lost IS (budget) high and performance at target (Lesson 8.1) | Lost IS (budget) low |
| **Add broad match** | All four conditions met (Lesson 3.3) | Weak tracking, low volume, few negatives |
| **Switch to PMax** | Data, feed and creative are ready (Lesson 6.2) | It would be your first campaign |
| **Enable auto-apply** | ❌ Almost never (Lesson 1.3) | Always decline |
| **Loosen the tCPA/tROAS target** | You are rank-limited and profitable (Lesson 8.1) | You are already at your profit ceiling |
| **Add more headlines** | You have really different angles (Lesson 4.1) | Only to raise Ad Strength |
| **Enable automatically created assets** | You will review them weekly | You will not |
| **Adopt a new campaign type** | You have capacity to test properly | It is a distraction from a bigger opportunity |

### How to decline well

You will decline recommendations. Do it in a way that preserves the relationship.

❌ "No, that is bad advice."
✅ "Thanks — I have looked at that. Our Lost IS to budget is only 4%, so I do not think extra budget would find much more volume. What I would find useful instead is help with [specific issue]."

**Show your reasoning and redirect to a real need.** Reps respond well to advertisers who clearly know their own account.

⚠️ **Never accept a recommendation you do not understand, out of politeness.** It is your client's money.

## Getting Betas

Beta access is really one of the most valuable things a rep provides.

### How betas are allocated

Google runs limited tests before general release. Access typically depends on:
- Relevant spend in the relevant product area
- A rep who advocates for you
- Willingness to provide feedback
- Sometimes a specific vertical or market

### How to increase your chances

**1. Ask directly.** "Are there any betas relevant to our account?" Many advertisers never ask.

**2. Be specific about what you want.** "We are interested in anything relating to offline conversion measurement" is more actionable than "anything new".

**3. Provide feedback when you get access.** Reps advocate for advertisers who make them look good. Structured, honest feedback does that.

**4. Have the technical capability.** Betas often need setup work. If you cannot set up, you will not be offered them again.

### The caution

⚠️ **Betas are unfinished products.**

- They can change or be withdrawn without notice
- They may have bugs
- Reporting may be incomplete
- Support may be limited

**Never put a client's core performance at risk on a beta.** Test on a small budget, in a contained campaign, with a clear rollback plan.

**And tell the client.** Running an unreleased product on their account without their knowledge is not acceptable.

## Escalation

When something is really stuck, escalation is what a rep is most valuable for.

### What can be escalated

- Policy decisions that appear incorrect
- Suspensions after standard appeals have failed (Lesson 1.5)
- Technical bugs affecting delivery or reporting
- Billing disputes
- Merchant Center issues resistant to normal fixes

### How to escalate effectively

Use the same structure as Lesson 1.5.

**1. Set up that you have exhausted normal channels.** "We have submitted two appeals through the standard process, on [dates]. Both were declined without specific reasons."

**2. State the issue in one sentence.**

**3. Provide the evidence.** Screenshots, account IDs, dates, URLs.

**4. State the business impact.** "This has stopped £4,200 of daily spend for six days." Impact justifies priority.

**5. State exactly what you want.** "A manual review by the policy team, with a specific reason if the decision is upheld."

**6. Be calm and factual.** Emotional escalations get slower results, not faster.

### What to expect

- Escalation takes time — days, sometimes weeks
- It does not guarantee the outcome you want
- **A rep cannot overturn a policy decision themselves.** They can get it looked at by someone who can.
- Repeated escalation of weak cases reduces your credibility for the strong ones

⚠️ **Save escalation for cases that really matter.** A rep has limited escalation capital. Spend it well.

## When You Have No Rep

Most advertisers, especially in smaller markets, have no rep. You are not helpless.

### What is available to everyone

**1. Chat and email support.** Enough for billing and simple issues. Use the Lesson 0.7 message structure.

**2. The Google Ads Community forums.** Product Experts there are experienced advertisers, some with escalation paths. A well-written post with real detail gets real help.

**3. Google Ads Help documentation.** Really complete, and more current than most third-party content.

**4. Skillshop.** Google's free training and certification platform.

**5. Official announcements and the Google Ads blog.** Product changes are announced publicly before most reps mention them.

### The practical strategy for smaller agencies

1. **Consolidate accounts under one MCC** to become visible (Lesson 10.1)
2. **Get certified** — it is free and it is a partner programme need
3. **Grow one account visibly** — growth attracts attention more than absolute size
4. **Use the forums well** — a good reputation there is really useful
5. **Do not wait for a rep to be effective.** Everything in this course works without one.

## 📖 Real-World Example: Getting a Rep From Rawalpindi

Bilal had no Google contact for his first two years. His accounts were managed entirely through the interface and the help documentation.

### The situation

- 14 clients
- Combined monthly spend: about £61,000
- Scattered across 14 separate logins, several of them accounts the clients had created themselves
- No MCC structure at first

**Nobody at Google knew he existed.**

### What he changed

**Step 1: Consolidation.** Following Lesson 10.1, he linked every account to a properly structured MCC.

**Combined spend became visible as a single portfolio: £61,000/month.** The same money, now legible.

**Step 2: Certification.** He and his two colleagues completed the Search, Measurement and Shopping certifications. Free, and a partner programme need.

**Step 3: Growth.** Over the following six months, combined spend grew to £94,000 — partly from new clients, partly because the work in Modules 7 and 8 made existing accounts scale profitably.

**Step 4: The partner programme.** He applied once the what you need were met.

**Three months later, he was assigned a partner manager.**

### The first call

His partner manager arrived with a 22-slide deck. Slide 4 recommended enabling auto-apply recommendations across all accounts.

**Bilal had sent an agenda beforehand.** It contained three items:

1. A measurement problem: offline conversion uploads showing a 40% match gap on one account
2. A question about a Merchant Center suspension that had been appealed twice
3. A request: any betas relating to offline conversion measurement

**The call went through his agenda first**, then the deck.

**Outcomes:**
- Item 1: introduced to a measurement specialist. A 45-minute call two weeks later found a phone number formatting problem (Lesson 7.4) that had been causing the match gap for months.
- Item 2: escalated. Reinstated in nine days after two failed standard appeals.
- Item 3: no beta immediately, but he was added to a list and received one four months later.
- The auto-apply recommendation: **declined**, with reasoning. The partner manager accepted it without argument.

### What the relationship produced over 18 months

| Benefit | Value |
|---|---|
| Measurement specialist calls (3) | Fixed two significant tracking problems |
| Escalations (5) | 4 resolved favourably, 1 upheld |
| Beta access (2) | One useful, one withdrawn before it mattered |
| Advance notice of product changes | Planning time on two significant changes |
| Training for his team | Free, and really useful |

### What it did not produce

- Preferential treatment in the auction. **Reps cannot influence auction outcomes.** Anyone who suggests otherwise is wrong.
- Automatic policy approvals
- Better prices

⚠️ **Be clear on this.** A rep gets you access to people and information. They do not get you a discount or an advantage in the auction. Any content claiming otherwise is misleading.

### The recommendations he declined

Over 18 months, he estimated he accepted roughly 40% of recommendations and declined 60%.

**Notable declines:**
- Auto-apply recommendations — three times, each time politely
- Switching a lead generation client to PMax as their primary campaign, when their offline conversion loop was not yet built
- Increasing budget on an account with 4% Lost IS to budget
- Adding broad match to an account with weak conversion tracking

**Every one of those declines protected a client.** And declining them with clear reasoning, rather than ignoring them, kept the relationship productive.

**His summary:** *"The rep relationship is worth having. It is not worth doing whatever they suggest. Those are two different things, and a lot of agencies confuse them."*

## 📌 Key Takeaways

- Reps provide escalation, betas, specialists and advance notice. They cannot influence the auction.
- Reps have targets around spend and product adoption. Evaluate every recommendation on its merits.
- Consolidate accounts under one MCC to become visible. Combined spend and growth both attract attention.
- Bring your own agenda to every call, and send it in advance. Otherwise you spend the call on theirs.
- Decline recommendations with clear reasoning. It protects your client and it strengthens the relationship.

## 🎯 Expert Challenge

Build a structured Google relationship management process.

- Design the standing agenda template for rep calls, with sections for your questions, your asks, and their recommendations — in that order.
- Build the recommendation evaluation log: every recommendation received, your decision, the reasoning, and the outcome if set up. After a year, this tells you objectively how good the advice has been, which is far more useful than an impression.
- Design the escalation process: what qualifies, the evidence pack needed, and the tracking of outcomes. Escalation capital is limited and should be spent on purpose.
- Build the beta evaluation framework: how you assess whether a beta is worth the setup cost and the risk, and the client disclosure and rollback plan.
- Design the visibility strategy if you do not have a rep: MCC consolidation, certification, growth trajectory, and a realistic timeline.
- Then, after twelve months, review your recommendation log. Calculate what percentage of accepted recommendations produced the predicted result. That number should inform how much weight you give future advice — from Google, from anyone, and from yourself.
