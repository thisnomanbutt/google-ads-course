## Why the Audit Matters Commercially

The audit is not just a diagnostic. It is:

**1. Your sales tool.** A free audit that finds PKR 340,000 of monthly waste sells better than any proposal you could write.

**2. Your onboarding process.** From Lesson 10.1, every new client goes through it.

**3. Your quality control.** Run it quarterly on existing clients. Things decay.

**4. Your intellectual property.** After a hundred audits, your framework is really valuable and really yours.

**5. Your credibility.** It shows competence more convincingly than any claim.

**Think of a building surveyor's report.** Anyone can say a house looks fine. A surveyor produces a structured document finding every issue, its severity, and the cost to fix. Nobody argues with the surveyor, because the process is visible.

## The Nine Sections

The full checklist is in `templates/100-point-account-audit.md`. Here is the structure and the reasoning.

### Section 1: Business & Economics (10 points)
*From Module 0*

Before anything technical. Margin, breakeven ROAS, maximum CAC, lead close rates, offer strength.

⚠️ **Most auditors skip this and go straight to the account.** It is the most valuable section, because an account cannot be judged without knowing what a customer is worth.

### Section 2: Account Foundations (10 points)
*From Modules 1–2*

Currency, timezone, auto-apply settings, access, billing, structure, naming, policy risk.

### Section 3: Conversion Tracking (15 points)
*From Modules 2 and 7*

**The largest single section, on purpose.** Everything downstream depends on measurement being right.

Primary versus secondary actions, values, Conversion Linker, Enhanced Conversions, GCLID capture, cross-domain, offline conversions, consent handling, and a live test.

### Section 4: Campaign Structure (10 points)
*From Modules 1 and 3*

Splitting logic, conversion volume per campaign, brand separation, intent clustering, landing page mapping.

### Section 5: Keywords & Targeting (12 points)
*From Module 3*

Intent distribution, match type strategy, negatives, n-gram analysis, search term hygiene, location settings.

### Section 6: Ads & Assets (12 points)
*From Module 4*

Headline count and variety, pinning, asset completeness, images, Quality Score components, message match.

### Section 7: Bidding & Budget (10 points)
*From Modules 5 and 10*

Strategy appropriateness, target derivation, learning phase status, impression share, pacing.

### Section 8: Channels & Audiences (11 points)
*From Modules 6 and 12*

Channel appropriateness for the data level, PMax controls, feed quality, audience structure, Customer Match, exclusions.

### Section 9: Measurement & Reporting (10 points)
*From Modules 8, 12 and 13*

Profit visibility, reporting quality, incrementality evidence, testing discipline, data warehouse.

**Total: 100 points.**

## Scoring

### The scale

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Pass** | Correctly configured, no action needed |
| **Partial** | Present but not best |
| **Fail** | Missing, broken, or actively harmful |
| **N/A** | Really not applicable |

### The account score

```
Score = (Passes + half the Partials) ÷ (100 − N/A items) × 100
```

**Typical results:**

| Score | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Below 40 | Serious problems throughout. Large opportunity. |
| 40–60 | Common for an account managed without a framework |
| 60–75 | Competently managed |
| 75–85 | Well managed by a good practitioner |
| Above 85 | Rare. Usually shows real expertise. |

⚠️ **The score is a communication device, not the point.** Nobody makes money from a score. The value is in the prioritised findings.

## Prioritising by Money, Not Severity

**This is what separates a useful audit from a list of complaints.**

Every finding gets an estimated monthly money impact.

### How to estimate

| Finding type | Estimation method |
|---|---|
| Wasted spend | Directly measurable from reports |
| Location targeting error | Spend outside the target area, from the User Locations report |
| Missing Enhanced Conversions | Estimate 10–20% conversion recovery × margin |
| Poor Quality Score | Model the CPC change × click volume (Lesson 4.5) |
| Budget-limited campaigns | Lost IS budget % × current conversions × margin |
| Loss-making products | Their spend minus their margin (Lesson 6.1) |
| No offline conversions | Hardest — use a comparable case, stated as an estimate |

⚠️ **Be conservative and label estimates as estimates.** An audit claiming PKR 2 million of monthly opportunity that delivers PKR 300,000 destroys trust permanently.

**Better to promise PKR 300,000 and deliver PKR 480,000.**

### The findings table

| # | Finding | Section | Monthly impact | Effort | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Location set to Presence-or-Interest; 34% of spend outside service area | 5 | **PKR 41,600** | 15 min | 🔴 Do today |
| 2 | Nine loss-making products in Shopping | 8 | PKR 38,000 | 2 hrs | 🔴 |
| 3 | No Enhanced Conversions | 3 | PKR 31,000 est. | 1 day | 🟠 |
| 4 | Six Primary conversion actions | 3 | PKR 28,000 est. | 30 min | 🔴 |
| 5 | Quality Score 4.1 on top 20 keywords | 6 | PKR 24,000 est. | 6 weeks | 🟡 |

**Sort by impact. Not by section, not by severity, not by how clever the finding is.**

## Presenting the Audit

The findings are half the work. The presentation is the half that wins the client.

### Format: a recorded walkthrough

A 12–18 minute screen recording, sent with the written document.

**Why recorded rather than live:**
- They watch it when they have attention, not when you are available
- They can rewatch and share it internally
- You can prepare properly
- It scales — the same effort serves every prospect

### The structure

**0:00–1:00 — What you did**
> "I have spent four hours going through your account against a 100-point framework. I found 23 issues. Three of them are costing you money today. Let me show you the three."

**No credentials, no company history.** Get to their account within sixty seconds.

**1:00–5:00 — The three biggest findings, on screen**

Show the actual account. Point at the actual number.

> "This is your locations report. This column is where people physically were. PKR 41,600 of last month's spend went to people outside Lahore. Here is the setting causing it. It takes fifteen seconds to change."

**Specific. Visual. Quantified.**

**5:00–12:00 — The remaining significant findings, grouped**

Faster now. Group related items.

**12:00–15:00 — The prioritised plan**

> "If you fix nothing else, fix these three. That is about PKR 110,000 a month, and it takes a day. The rest is a three-month programme."

**15:00–17:00 — What you would do**

Only now, briefly, what working together looks like.

**17:00–18:00 — The close**

> "The document has everything, with the workings. Fix the first three yourself if you prefer — the instructions are in there. If you want help with the rest, I am happy to talk."

⚠️ **Offer the findings without conditions.** An audit held hostage to a contract reads as a sales trick. Giving away the first three fixes is what makes the rest credible.

## The Presentation Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Criticising the previous agency
❌ *"Whoever set this up clearly did not know what they were doing."*

**Never.** The client chose that agency. Criticising them criticises the client's judgement, and you sound insecure.

✅ *"This is a common setup — the default settings cause it. Here is what to change."*

### Mistake 2: Leading with jargon
❌ *"Your impression share lost to rank is 58% and your Quality Score components show below-average landing page experience."*

✅ *"You are missing about six in ten of the searches you could appear for, because Google rates your pages below your competitors'. That is fixable and it costs nothing extra."*

### Mistake 3: Listing all 23 findings equally
Attention collapses after item four. **Lead with three. Group the rest.**

### Mistake 4: Overstating the opportunity
The fastest way to lose a client at month three. **Be conservative.**

### Mistake 5: No numbers
An audit without money attached is an opinion.

### Mistake 6: Making it about you
Nobody cares about your certifications. They care about the PKR 41,600.

### Mistake 7: Vagueness about effort
"This needs optimisation" tells them nothing. **Say how long each fix takes.**

## Running It on Existing Clients

Not just a sales tool. Quarterly, on every account.

**Why things decay:**
- Websites change and break tracking (Lesson 2.5)
- New products enter feeds without margin labels (Lesson 6.1)
- Someone enables auto-apply
- Targets set months ago stop matching the market
- New campaigns are built without following the standard

⚠️ **From Lesson 10.1: when Bilal ran his audit retroactively on 14 existing clients, every single one had problems.** These were accounts he had personally built and managed.

**Competent people forget things when they are busy. The framework is what catches it.**

## 📖 Real-World Example: The Audit That Won a Retainer

Bilal was invited to pitch for a UK e-commerce account: £42,000 monthly spend, three agencies competing.

**The other two sent proposals.** Case studies, team credentials, method slides, a fee schedule.

**Bilal sent an audit.**

### What he did

Four hours in the account, working through the 100-point framework. He asked for read-only access and one 20-minute call to get the margin figures (Section 1).

**Score: 51/100.** Not unusual.

**He found 27 issues.** Here were the top five.

| # | Finding | Monthly impact | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Conversion values include 20% VAT — every ROAS target is 20% wrong | **£8,400** | 2 hrs |
| 2 | Checkout on a separate domain, no cross-domain tracking. 41% of orders attributed to Direct | **£6,900** | 1 day |
| 3 | 14 products with negative contribution in Shopping | **£4,100** | 3 hrs |
| 4 | PMax with no brand exclusions, absorbing 34% brand traffic | **£3,800** | 30 min |
| 5 | Location set to Presence-or-Interest; 19% of spend outside the UK | **£2,600** | 15 min |

**Total found: £25,800 a month.** He estimated conservatively and said so.

### The recording

Fourteen minutes.

**Minute 1:** *"I spent four hours in your account. I found 27 things. Five of them are costing you money right now, and I will show you those. The other 22 are in the document."*

**Minutes 1–4:** the VAT finding, on screen. He showed the conversion value arriving in Google, then the same order in their Shopify admin. **The numbers were visibly 20% apart.**

> "Every ROAS target you have set is based on a number that is 20% too high. You are not making the margin you think you are. This is a two-hour fix and it is the single most valuable thing in this document."

**Minutes 4–9:** the other four findings, each shown live in the account.

**Minutes 9–12:** the grouped remainder.

**Minutes 12–13:** the plan.

> "Fix items one, four and five yourself this week. That is about £14,800 a month and roughly three hours of work. The instructions are in the document, with screenshots. Items two and three need more time — that is where I would start if we worked together."

**Minutes 13–14:** one paragraph about working together. No fee discussion.

### The outcome

The client called the next morning.

Their first question: *"Why are you telling us how to fix things for free?"*

His answer: *"Because if you fix them and I was wrong, you have lost three hours. If I am right, you will know I can find things nobody else found. Either way you are better off."*

**They fixed items 1, 4 and 5 that week.** Monthly profit rose by £13,200 — close to his estimate.

**They hired him in week two**, at a fee 40% above what they had paid the previous agency.

### Why he won

The other two agencies had sent proposals about themselves. He had sent findings about them.

**The client's words:** *"The other two told us they were good. You showed us."*

### The part that matters most

⚠️ **He gave away the three easiest fixes, worth £14,800 a month, with no commitment.**

Many people would consider that a mistake — giving away the value that should have been the first month's work.

**It was the reason he won.** Giving away real value with no conditions is the strongest possible proof that you have more of it.

**He now runs the audit on every prospect.** His conversion rate from audit to client is roughly one in three, against roughly one in eight from written proposals.

## 📌 Key Takeaways

- The audit is a sales tool, an onboarding process, a quality control system and your intellectual property.

## 🎯 Expert Challenge

Turn the audit into a business system.

- Adapt the 100 points to your market. Some will not apply; add points specific to the industries you serve.
- Build the money estimation model for each finding type, with a documented method so estimates are consistent across auditors and defensible when challenged.
- Create the audit deliverable template: the document, the findings table, the priority plan, and the recording structure — so any team member produces the same quality.
- Design the quarterly audit programme for existing clients, with a tracked score over time. A client's score rising from 51 to 84 over a year is one of the most persuasive retention arguments available.
- Build the findings database: every finding across every audit, tagged by type. After fifty audits this tells you which problems are most common, which are most valuable, and where your own process should improve.
- Then measure the commercial result. Track your conversion rate from audit to client, and the accuracy of your impact estimates. Being right about the money is what turns a one-off win into a reputation.
