## What a Responsive Search Ad Is

A Responsive Search Ad (RSA) is not one ad. It is a **box of parts** that Google assembles differently for each auction.

You provide:
- Up to **15 headlines** (30 characters each)
- Up to **4 descriptions** (90 characters each)

Google picks **3 headlines and 2 descriptions** for each individual auction, choosing the combination it predicts will perform best for that specific person.

**Think of a wardrobe.** You do not decide the exact outfit for every day of the year in advance. You fill the wardrobe with good pieces that work together, and choose based on the weather each morning. Google is choosing the outfit; you are stocking the wardrobe.

**This changes your job.** You are no longer writing one perfect ad. You are supplying a set of strong, varied parts that combine well.

---

## The 15-Headline Framework

Do not write 15 variations of the same idea. Cover 15 **different angles**. Here is the framework.

Use Sana's `Problem - No Electricity` cluster as the running example.

| # | Slot purpose | Example (max 30 chars) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Exact keyword match** | Water Filter No Electricity |
| 2 | **Keyword variation** | Non-Electric Water Purifier |
| 3 | **Main benefit** | Clean Water During Load-Shed |
| 4 | **Mechanism** (Lesson 0.2) | Korean Nano-Membrane Filter |
| 5 | **Proof / social** | 40,000 Karachi Families Use It |
| 6 | **Risk removal** | 30-Day Money Back Guarantee |
| 7 | **Price / offer** | From PKR 5,000, Free Delivery |
| 8 | **Speed** | Delivered in 48 Hours |
| 9 | **Effort removal** | No Plumber, No Installation |
| 10 | **Objection handler** | No Power? No Problem. |
| 11 | **Call to action** | Order Today, Pay on Delivery |
| 12 | **Urgency** (only if real) | Limited Stock This Month |
| 13 | **Local trust** | Karachi-Based, Same-Day Support |
| 14 | **Differentiator** | Removes 99.9% of Bacteria |
| 15 | **Question format** | Tired of Boiling Water Daily? |

### Why this framework works

Google can now build many really different ads:

- Keyword + benefit + call to action
- Proof + risk removal + price
- Question + mechanism + speed

Each combination speaks to a different person. That is the entire point of an RSA.

### Character limits are hard limits

30 characters for headlines. 90 for descriptions. Count them. A headline that gets cut off looks unprofessional and lowers CTR.

**Tip:** aim for 25–28 characters. Google sometimes needs room, and shorter headlines often read better on mobile.

## The Four Descriptions

Descriptions get less attention than headlines but they carry the detail.

| # | Purpose | Example (max 90 chars) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Full offer summary** | Clean drinking water without electricity. Korean nano-membrane removes 99.9% of bacteria. |
| 2 | **Risk and trust** | 30-day money back guarantee. We collect it ourselves. Cash on delivery available. |
| 3 | **Proof and specifics** | Trusted by 40,000 families in Karachi. Delivered in 48 hours across the city. |
| 4 | **Call to action** | Order online in 2 minutes. Free delivery. Pay when it arrives at your door. |

**Rules:**
- Write complete sentences. Descriptions are read, not scanned.
- Include your main keyword in at least one description.
- Never repeat the same information in all four.

## Pinning: The Feature That Helps and Hurts

Pinning locks a specific headline into a specific position (1, 2 or 3).

### What pinning does to your ad

If you pin one headline to position 1, Google can no longer test other headlines there. You have reduced the number of possible combinations — sometimes by a huge amount.

**Ad Strength will drop.** That is expected, and not automatically bad.

### When pinning is correct

1. **Legal or regulatory what you need.** Financial services, healthcare, or anything where a disclaimer must appear.
2. **Brand name must always show.** Common for well-known brands.
3. **A claim that must never appear without context.** For example, a price that must always sit beside "starting from".
4. **Preventing a policy problem.** If two headlines together could read as a misleading claim (Lesson 1.5).

### When pinning is wrong

1. "I think this headline is best." You are guessing. Let the data decide.
2. To force a keyword into position 1. Google already does this when it helps.
3. Because Ad Strength went down. Ad Strength is a guide, not a score to maximise.

### If you must pin, pin in pairs or threes

Pin **two or three** headlines to the same position rather than one. Google can then still test between them.

```
Position 1: Pin headlines 1, 2 and 10  (Google tests among these three)
Position 2: unpinned (Google tests all remaining)
Position 3: unpinned
```

This keeps control and keeps testing.

## Ad Strength: What It Really Means

Google rates each RSA: Poor, Average, Good, Excellent.

**What it measures:** how much variety and relevance your assets have. It is a *diagnostic*, not a performance prediction.

**What it does NOT measure:** whether your ads actually make money.

### The honest position

- Moving from Poor to Good usually **does** help, because Poor normally means too few assets or too much repetition.
- Moving from Good to Excellent often makes **no measurable difference** to results.
- Chasing Excellent by adding weak, generic headlines actively hurts you.

**Target: Good.** Aim higher only if you can do it with really strong assets.

⚠️ **Never add a bad headline to raise Ad Strength.** Google will serve it, and it will cost you money.

---

## Reading Asset Performance Ratings

Google rates each individual headline and description:

| Rating | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Best** | Performing in the top group | Keep. Write more like this. |
| **Good** | Performing acceptably | Keep |
| **Low** | Underperforming | Replace |
| **Learning** | Not enough data yet | Wait |
| **Pending** | Not yet reviewed | Wait |

### How to read this correctly

⚠️ **Ratings are relative, not absolute.** "Low" means low *compared to your other assets in this ad group*. If all 15 headlines are excellent, some will still be rated Low. That does not mean they are bad.

⚠️ **You need volume.** Below roughly 500 impressions per asset, ratings are unreliable. Do not act on early data.

⚠️ **Pinned assets often show "Learning" forever.** They have no comparison group.

### The replacement routine

Once a month:
1. Sort assets by rating
2. Find anything rated **Low** with at least 500 impressions
3. Replace **2–3 at a time**, never all of them
4. Write the replacement using a *different angle*, not a small edit of the same idea
5. Wait 3–4 weeks before judging

**Why only 2–3 at a time?** Because if you replace ten and performance changes, you learn nothing about which change mattered.

---

## How Many Ads Per Ad Group?

**Answer: one strong RSA, plus a second if you are testing something specific.**

The old advice of "3 ads per ad group" came from the era of Expanded Text Ads, when each ad was fixed. An RSA already tests dozens of combinations internally. A second RSA competes with the first for the same impressions and splits your data.

**Run two RSAs only when:** you are testing a really different *strategy* — a different offer, a different core promise — not different wording.

## 📖 Real-World Example: Rewriting Dr. Ayesha's Ads

Her original RSA had 6 headlines. All of them said roughly the same thing.

**Before:**
1. Best Dentist in Lahore
2. Top Dental Clinic Lahore
3. Expert Dentist Lahore
4. Professional Dental Care
5. Quality Dental Treatment
6. Affordable Dentist Lahore

Ad Strength: **Poor.** CTR: 3.1%.

Look at what Google had to work with. Every combination said "we are a good dentist in Lahore". Three of those together is not an ad; it is a repeated claim. And every competitor said the same (Lesson 0.2).

**After — 15 headlines, 15 angles**, built for her `Problem - Fear` cluster:

1. Scared of the Dentist?
2. Pain-Free Dentist in Lahore
3. We Numb Before the Needle
4. You Won't Feel the Injection
5. Free 15-Minute Check-Up
6. Written Price Before We Start
7. Price Never Changes. Promise.
8. No Treatment Pushed on Visit 1
9. 2,400 Nervous Patients Treated
10. Gulberg Clinic, Easy Parking
11. Book Online in 60 Seconds
12. Open Saturdays and Evenings
13. See Patient Videos First
14. Dental Anxiety Specialists
15. Your First Visit Is Just a Chat

**Descriptions:**
1. "We use numbing gel before every injection, so you never feel the needle. Truly pain-free."
2. "Free 15-minute check-up. We give you a written price before starting. It never changes."
3. "2,400 nervous patients treated in Gulberg. Watch their videos before you decide to come."
4. "Book online in 60 seconds. Evening and Saturday appointments available. No pressure, ever."

Ad Strength: **Good.**

**Results after 6 weeks:**

| Metric | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| CTR | 3.1% | 7.4% |
| Quality Score | 5 | 8 |
| Average CPC | PKR 47 | PKR 31 |
| Conversion rate | 2.2% | 4.9% |
| Cost per booking | PKR 4,200 | PKR 1,350 |

**Three things happened at once, and it is worth understanding all three:**

1. **CTR more than doubled** because the ads finally said something no competitor said.
2. **CPC dropped 34%** because higher CTR raised Quality Score, which lowered her price per click (Lesson 1.1).
3. **Conversion rate more than doubled** because the ad promise matched the landing page promise (Lesson 0.5).

She paid less per click **and** got more from each click. That is the compounding effect of good creative, and it is why copywriting is not a "soft" skill in paid media.

### Asset ratings after 8 weeks

Google's data revealed something she would not have guessed.

- **Best:** #3 "We Numb Before the Needle", #7 "Price Never Changes. Promise.", #1 "Scared of the Dentist?"
- **Low:** #4 "Professional Dental Care" (a leftover), #12 "Open Saturdays and Evenings", #14 "Dental Anxiety Specialists"

She had expected #14 to be a winner — it sounded expert and specific. It was rated Low. Meanwhile "Scared of the Dentist?", which she had almost cut for being too blunt, was among the best.

**The data disagreed with the expert.** That is normal, and it is exactly why RSAs test instead of assuming.

She replaced the three Low assets with new angles: "No Judgement About Your Teeth", "Bring a Friend to Your First Visit", "Tell Us Your Fear First".

## 📌 Key Takeaways

- An RSA is a box of parts. Your job is stocking it with varied, strong assets — not writing one perfect ad.
- Use the 15-slot framework so each headline covers a different angle, not a reworded version of the same one.
- Pin only for legal, brand or policy reasons. If you must pin, pin two or three to the same position.
- Ad Strength is a diagnostic, not a target. Aim for Good; never add weak assets to reach Excellent.
- Replace only 2–3 Low-rated assets at a time, and only after 500+ impressions.

## ☑️ Self-Assessment

1. How many headlines do your current ads have? Are they 15 different angles or 6 similar ones?
2. Have you pinned anything? Can you justify it with a legal, brand or policy reason?
3. Which of your assets are rated Low? When did you last replace any?
4. Does your ad mention your mechanism (Lesson 0.2), or just claims?
5. Read your ad and your landing page headline together. Do they match?

## 💪 Practical Exercise (40 minutes)

Write a full 15-headline RSA for one intent cluster.

1. Pick one cluster from your Lesson 3.5 map.
2. Work down the 15-slot framework, writing one headline per slot. Count characters.
3. Write four descriptions, each with a different job.
4. Read three random combinations aloud. Do they all make sense as complete ads?
5. Check every claim against Lesson 1.5. Can you prove each one?
6. Compare your ad headline to the landing page headline. Fix any mismatch.

## 🎯 Expert Challenge

Build a creative testing system, not just a single ad.

- Create a message hypothesis document for one business: five separate positioning angles, each with the customer belief it targets and the evidence supporting it.
- Build an RSA for the strongest angle, then design the test that would prove or disprove a second angle. Decide whether that test is a second RSA, a separate ad group, or a Google Experiment (Lesson 13.1).
- Define the sample size you need before judging. Use your conversion rate and desired confidence — most people judge far too early.
- Build the asset rotation calendar: which assets get reviewed when, and the rule for when a Low rating triggers replacement versus investigation.
- Create the winning-message library: a document where every proven headline across all your accounts is recorded, with the industry and the result. After a year this becomes the most valuable asset you own.
