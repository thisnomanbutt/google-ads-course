## Why Assets Are the Cheapest Win in Google Ads

Remember the Ad Rank formula from Lesson 1.1:

> **Ad Rank = Bid × Quality × Asset impact + Context**

**Asset impact is a direct multiplier.** Better assets raise your Ad Rank without costing you a rupee more per click.

Assets also do two other things:

1. **They make your ad physically bigger.** An ad with sitelinks, callouts and images takes up far more of the screen than a plain text ad. On mobile, a full ad can push competitors below the fold entirely.
2. **They give people more reasons to click, and more entry points.** Someone who is not ready to buy might click "See Prices" instead of leaving.

**Think of a market stall.** Two sellers have the same product at the same price. One has a bare table. The other has a canopy, a price board, product samples and photos of happy customers. Which stall do people walk toward?

Assets are the canopy. They cost you nothing extra.

## The Asset Types, Ranked by Importance

### 🥇 Tier 1: Build these on every campaign, always

**Sitelinks** — extra links below your ad. The single highest impact asset.
**Callouts** — short text snippets that cannot be clicked. Fast to write, always worth it.
**Structured snippets** — a labelled list, such as "Services: Implants, Braces, Whitening".

### 🥈 Tier 2: Build these when they apply

**Call asset** — your phone number, tappable on mobile. Essential for service businesses.
**Location asset** — your address and map pin. Essential for local businesses.
**Image assets** — pictures beside your text ad. Strong impact, especially on mobile.
**Business name and logo** — makes your ad look set up and trustworthy.

### 🥉 Tier 3: Situational but powerful

**Price asset** — a small table of your products or services with prices.
**Promotion asset** — highlights a specific offer with a sale tag.
**Lead form asset** — collects a lead directly in the search results.
**App asset** — for app installs.

## Sitelinks: The Highest Impact Asset

Sitelinks are extra links below your main ad. Each one goes to a different page.

### The rules

- **Minimum 4 per campaign.** Google usually needs at least four before it shows any.
- **Build 6–8.** More options means Google can choose the best combination.
- **Each needs a headline (25 chars) and two description lines (35 chars each).**
- **Always fill in the descriptions.** Sitelinks with descriptions take much more space on the page. Most advertisers skip this. Do not.

### What makes a good sitelink

❌ **Bad sitelinks** (generic, useless):
- Home
- About Us
- Contact
- Services

Nobody searching for a water filter wants to read your "About Us" page.

✅ **Good sitelinks** (specific, useful, action-oriented):

| Headline | Description line 1 | Description line 2 |
|---|---|---|
| See All Prices | Filters from PKR 5,000 | Free delivery in Karachi |
| No Electricity Models | Works during load-shedding | Gravity powered, no plumber |
| Customer Videos | Watch 40,000 families | Real homes, real results |
| 30-Day Guarantee | Full money back | We collect it ourselves |
| Book Free Water Test | We test your tap water | Free, no obligation |
| Delivery in 48 Hours | Across Karachi | Cash on delivery available |

### The strategy behind sitelinks

Each sitelink should answer a **different objection or serve a different intent**.

Someone who is price-sensitive clicks "See All Prices". Someone who is worried about risk clicks "30-Day Guarantee". Someone who does not believe you clicks "Customer Videos".

**One ad. Six different doorways.**

### Where to add them

You can add sitelinks at account, campaign or ad group level. **Campaign level is usually right** — specific enough to be relevant, broad enough to be manageable.

Use ad-group-level sitelinks only when a cluster needs really different links.

## Callouts: Quick Wins

Callouts are short phrases (25 characters max) that appear below your ad. They cannot be clicked.

### The rules
- Add **at least 6**, ideally 8–10
- Keep each to 12–20 characters where possible — short ones fit better
- No punctuation at the end
- Each should be a separate benefit

### Good callout examples

**For Sana:**
- Free Delivery Karachi
- Cash on Delivery
- 30-Day Money Back
- No Electricity Needed
- 40,000+ Customers
- 48-Hour Delivery
- Free Water Testing
- 2-Year Warranty

**For Dr. Ayesha:**
- Pain-Free Treatment
- Free First Check-Up
- Written Price Upfront
- Open Saturdays
- Easy Parking
- Evening Appointments
- 2,400 Patients Treated
- No Pressure Ever

**Time to write:** 10 minutes. **Impact:** consistently measurable. This is the best effort-to-result ratio in Google Ads.

## Structured Snippets

A structured snippet is a labelled list. You choose a header, then list values.

**Format:** `Header: Value 1, Value 2, Value 3...`

**Examples:**
- `Services: Implants, Braces, Whitening, Root Canal, Cleaning`
- `Types: RO Filters, UV Purifiers, Gravity Filters, Commercial Units`
- `Brands: Philips, Kent, Aquaguard, PureIt`

**Rules:**
- Minimum 3 values, aim for 6–8
- Use the header that really fits. Do not force it.
- Add 2–3 different headers so Google can choose

## Image Assets: The One Most People Skip

Image assets place a picture beside your text ad on mobile and increasingly on desktop.

### Why they matter

A search results page is a wall of blue and black text. **One image breaks that wall.** On mobile especially, this is a significant visual advantage.

### The specifications

- **Square (1:1):** minimum 300×300 px, recommended 1200×1200
- **Landscape (1.91:1):** minimum 600×314 px, recommended 1200×628
- **Upload both formats.** Different placements use different shapes.
- Maximum file size: 5120 KB
- Add up to 20 images per campaign or ad group

### What works

✅ The product, clearly, filling most of the frame
✅ The product in real use
✅ Real people from the business — a dentist with a patient, a technician installing
✅ Bright, clean, high contrast

### What fails

❌ Text inside the image (Google restricts this and it looks cluttered)
❌ Logos as the main image
❌ Generic stock photos of foreign models — these actively reduce trust in local markets (Lesson 0.5)
❌ Dark, cluttered or low-resolution images
❌ Collages

⚠️ **Google may auto-generate images from your website.** Review these. Sometimes they pick something odd. You can remove auto-generated assets in the assets section.

## Business Name and Logo

Adding your business name and logo makes your ad look set up. This matters most for:

- New brands building trust
- Local businesses competing with large national ones
- Any market where scams are a real concern

**What you need:** logo must be square (1:1), minimum 128×128 px, and it must be your actual business logo.

Small effort. Meaningful trust signal.

## Call and Location Assets

### Call asset

Shows your phone number, tappable on mobile.

**Essential for:** clinics, home services, emergency services, B2B with a sales team.

**Set it up properly:**
- Use a **Google forwarding number** so calls are tracked as conversions
- Set the **schedule** to your actual answering hours. There is no point showing a phone number when nobody will answer.
- Set a **minimum call duration** for the conversion — 60 or 90 seconds — so short wrong-number calls do not count (Lesson 2.3)

### Location asset

Shows your address and a map pin. Needs a linked Google Business Profile (Lesson 2.2).

**Essential for:** any business people physically visit.

⚠️ **Make sure your business name, address and phone are identical** across the website, Business Profile and Google Ads. Inconsistency causes verification problems.

## Price and Promotion Assets

### Price asset

A small table showing services or products with prices.

**When to use it:**
- Your prices are competitive
- Price transparency filters out bad leads (Lesson 0.6)
- You want to pre-qualify before paying for the click

**When to avoid it:**
- Your prices are the highest in the market and your value is in quality
- Prices vary too much to state meaningfully

**Dr. Ayesha's price asset:**
| Item | Price | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Consultation | Free | 15-minute check-up, no obligation |
| Teeth Cleaning | From PKR 4,000 | Full scale and polish |
| Dental Implant | From PKR 85,000 | Including crown, 2 visits |
| Braces | From PKR 120,000 | Full treatment, monthly plan |

Notice what this does: it removes price-shoppers who cannot afford implants **before** she pays for their click. That is Lesson 0.6 applied at the ad level.

### Promotion asset

Highlights a specific offer with a sale tag. Use for real, time-limited promotions only.

⚠️ **A promotion asset that never expires is fake urgency, and fake urgency is a misrepresentation risk (Lesson 1.5).** Set real end dates.

## Reviewing Asset Performance

Google rates assets the same way it rates headlines: Best, Good, Low, Learning.

**Where to find it:** Campaigns → Assets → set the table view to show performance.

### The monthly review

1. Filter for assets rated **Low** with meaningful impressions
2. Replace **2–3 at a time** (same rule as Lesson 4.1)
3. Check which sitelinks get the most clicks — build more like those
4. Remove any auto-generated asset you would not have written yourself

### What "Low" often reveals

A sitelink rated Low is usually one of these:
- Generic ("About Us")
- Duplicating another sitelink
- Pointing to a page that does not match its promise

Fix the cause, not just the rating.

## 📖 Real-World Example: Assets Alone, No Other Changes

Bilal inherited a US home services account. The ads themselves were reasonable — 12 headlines, decent copy. But the assets section was almost empty:

- 2 sitelinks: "Home" and "Contact Us"
- 0 callouts
- 0 structured snippets
- 0 images
- Call asset present but with no schedule and no minimum duration

He spent **90 minutes** building assets. He changed nothing else. No new keywords, no bid changes, no landing page edits, no ad copy changes.

**What he built:**

**8 sitelinks**, each with full descriptions:
- Same-Day Service, Free Estimates, See Our Prices, 24/7 Emergency, Customer Reviews, Areas We Serve, Financing Available, Meet Our Technicians

**10 callouts:**
- Licensed & Insured, Same-Day Service, Free Estimates, 24/7 Emergency, 15 Years Experience, No Hidden Fees, Upfront Pricing, Financing Available, 500+ 5-Star Reviews, Family Owned

**3 structured snippets:**
- Services: AC Repair, Heating, Ducts, Installation, Maintenance
- Types: Residential, Commercial, Emergency, Scheduled
- Neighbourhoods: (8 local area names)

**12 images:** technicians at work, real vans with branding, before-and-after shots, the team photo. No stock images.

**Business name and logo** added.

**Call asset fixed:** forwarding number for tracking, schedule set to 7am–9pm, minimum duration 60 seconds.

**Results after 5 weeks:**

| Metric | Before | After | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| CTR | 4.2% | 7.1% | +69% |
| Average CPC | $6.80 | $5.40 | −21% |
| Impression share | 41% | 58% | +17 pts |
| Conversions | 84/month | 149/month | +77% |
| Cost per conversion | $71 | $42 | −41% |
| Monthly spend | $5,960 | $6,260 | +5% |

**Why did CPC fall while impression share rose?**

Higher CTR raised Quality Score. Better assets raised the asset impact component of Ad Rank. Both lowered his cost per click while raising his position (Lesson 1.1). He got more, better placements for slightly less money each.

**Ninety minutes of work. A 41% reduction in cost per conversion.**

The client asked what clever strategy he had used. The honest answer was: *"I filled in the boxes the last agency left empty."*

That opportunity exists in a surprising number of accounts. Check the assets section of every account you inherit — it is often the fastest win available.

## 📌 Key Takeaways

- Assets raise Ad Rank directly, without raising your bid. They are free performance.
- Sitelinks are the highest impact. Build 6–8, always with descriptions, each answering a different objection.
- Callouts take 10 minutes and always help. Add 8–10.
- Images break the wall of text, especially on mobile. Use real photos, never generic stock.
- Review asset ratings monthly and replace 2–3 Low performers at a time.

## 🎯 Expert Challenge

Design an asset strategy that varies by intent cluster.

- For each cluster from Lesson 3.5, decide which sitelinks best serve that specific intent. A price-focused cluster and a problem-focused cluster should not get identical sitelinks.
- Build an objection map: list the top eight reasons someone does not buy from this business, and assign each one to a sitelink, callout or image.
- Design the image testing plan: which visual angles you will test, how many impressions before judging, and how you will attribute lift to images rather than to other changes.
- Build the asset governance document: who approves images, how often assets are reviewed, and the rule for removing auto-generated assets.
- Finally, quantify it. Estimate the Ad Rank improvement from a full asset build using your current CTR and Quality Score, and calculate the annual value. Use this number to justify the 90 minutes to any client who thinks assets are cosmetic.
