## The Big Idea: Two Kinds of Advertising

Every campaign type does one of two jobs.

### 1. Demand capture
Someone already wants the thing. You appear at that moment.
*Example:* they search "emergency plumber lahore" at midnight.

### 2. Demand creation
Nobody was looking. You interrupt them with something interesting.
*Example:* a YouTube ad for a water filter shown to someone watching a cooking video.

**Think of two kinds of fishing.** In the first, you find a spot where hungry fish are already gathered and drop your line in. In the second, you throw food into empty water to attract fish, then wait.

The first is faster and cheaper per fish. The second is how you grow beyond the size of the pond.

**The critical rule: capture demand before you create it.** Always. If you cannot profit from people who are already searching for you, you will not profit from strangers.

## Every Campaign Type in One Line

| Campaign type | What it is | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Search** | Text ads on Google search results | Capture people searching right now |
| **Shopping** | Product photos with prices on search | Capture product searches with visual proof |
| **Performance Max (PMax)** | One campaign across all Google surfaces | Capture and create, controlled by AI |
| **Demand Gen** | Visual ads on YouTube, Discover and Gmail | Create demand with creative |
| **YouTube / Video** | Video ads on YouTube | Create demand and build memory |
| **Display** | Banner ads across millions of websites | Mostly remarketing; cheap reminders |
| **App** | Promote app installs and in-app actions | Grow app users |

## Search: The Foundation

Search is where you start. Always. Every time.

**Why:** the person told you what they want. There is no guessing. Intent is highest, and results appear fastest.

**Best for:** services, local businesses, lead generation, high-consideration products.

**Weakness:** limited by how many people search. If only 400 people a month search for your thing, Search alone cannot grow you.

**Beginner truth:** if Search does not work for your business, the problem is almost always the offer, the page or the tracking — not the channel.

---

## Shopping and Performance Max: For Products

**Shopping** shows your product photo, price and shop name directly in the results. For e-commerce, it usually beats text ads because people can see the item and the price before clicking.

Shopping needs a **product feed** in Merchant Center. The feed is a file listing every product with its title, price, image and stock. Feed quality decides Shopping performance more than bids do. That is Lesson 6.1.

**Performance Max** is Google's AI-run campaign. You give it your product feed, images, videos, headlines and a goal. It decides where to show: Search, Shopping, YouTube, Display, Gmail, Maps and Discover.

**PMax strengths:**
- Finds demand you did not know existed
- Very strong when you have good data and good creative
- Simple to launch

**PMax risks — read these carefully:**
- It hides much of what it is doing
- It can eat your brand searches and claim credit for sales you would have got free
- With weak data or weak creative, it burns money quietly
- It needs a real volume of conversions to learn

⚠️ **The rule for PMax:** never run it as your *first* campaign. Run Search first, get clean conversion data, then add PMax with brand exclusions. We control it properly in Lesson 6.3.

---

## Demand Gen and YouTube: For Growth

**Demand Gen** places visual and video ads in YouTube feeds, Discover and Gmail. Think of it as Google's answer to social media advertising.

**Best for:** businesses that have already maxed out Search and need new customers.

**It lives or dies on creative.** Your targeting matters far less than your video or image. A brilliant 15-second video with average targeting beats perfect targeting with a boring image, every time.

**YouTube / Video** campaigns build memory and trust. They rarely produce an immediate sale.

**The honest truth about YouTube:** it is measured over months, not days. If you judge a YouTube campaign on last-click sales after two weeks, you will turn it off and conclude it does not work. Both of those conclusions will be wrong.

Do not run YouTube until:
- Your Search account is profitable
- Your tracking is solid
- You can afford to spend for 8–12 weeks before judging

---

## Display: Use It Narrowly

Display shows banners across millions of websites. It is cheap and it is dangerous.

**Where it works:** remarketing. Showing a reminder to people who already visited your site.

**Where it usually fails:** targeting cold strangers by interest. Low quality placements, accidental clicks on mobile games, and invalid traffic can waste large amounts of money. We hunt this in Lesson 8.5.

**Simple rule for beginners:** use Display for remarketing only, until you are experienced.

## The Goal × Maturity Matrix

Now the practical part. Choose your channel based on two things: **what you want** and **how much data you have**.

### Step 1: Find your maturity level

| Level | Conversions per month | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Level 1: Starting** | Under 15 | AI has almost nothing to learn from |
| **Level 2: Learning** | 15–50 | Smart Bidding can begin to work |
| **Level 3: Scaling** | 50–200 | Automation performs well |
| **Level 4: Mature** | 200+ | Advanced strategies unlock |

### Step 2: Read your row

| | **Goal: Leads** | **Goal: E-commerce sales** | **Goal: Awareness** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L1: Under 15 conv/mo** | Search (exact + phrase), manual or Max Clicks. Fix tracking first. | Search brand + Standard Shopping. Feed quality first. | Do not run awareness yet. |
| **L2: 15–50 conv/mo** | Search + Max Conversions. Add remarketing. | Shopping + Search. Test one PMax with brand excluded. | Small remarketing video only. |
| **L3: 50–200 conv/mo** | Search + tCPA. Add Demand Gen. Start offline conversions. | PMax + Search + Shopping. Move to tROAS. | Demand Gen with strong creative. |
| **L4: 200+ conv/mo** | Full stack, value-based bidding, lead scoring feedback. | PMax profit tiers, POAS bidding, YouTube. | YouTube at scale, measured with lift studies. |

**How to use this:** find your box. Do what it says. Ignore everything else until you move up a level.

## The Correct Order to Add Channels

Follow this order. Do not skip steps.

1. **Brand Search** — people searching your business name. Cheapest conversions you will ever get. Protects you from competitors.
2. **High-intent non-brand Search** — "buy", "near me", "price", "book".
3. **Shopping** (if you sell products) — with a clean feed.
4. **Remarketing** — Search remarketing lists first, then Display.
5. **Performance Max** — once you have real conversion data and brand exclusions ready.
6. **Demand Gen** — once Search is at its ceiling and you have good creative.
7. **YouTube** — once you can wait 8–12 weeks to judge results.
8. **Broad match expansion** — once Smart Bidding has enough data to steer it.

Each step should be profitable before you move to the next.

### About brand campaigns

Some people argue you should not pay for your own name. Here is the balanced answer:

**Run a brand campaign when:**
- Competitors bid on your name (check Auction Insights)
- You have a common word as a business name
- You want to control the message and the landing page

**It matters less when:**
- Nobody bids on your name
- You already own the whole results page
- Budget is extremely tight and every rupee must find new customers

Test it honestly: pause brand for two weeks and watch total sales, not just Google Ads sales.

## The Most Expensive Beginner Mistake

Here it is: **launching PMax as your very first campaign because it is easy.**

Why it goes wrong:

- With no conversion data, the AI has nothing to learn from
- It spends across every surface at once, so you cannot tell what worked
- It absorbs your brand traffic and reports beautiful results that would have happened anyway
- You conclude Google Ads "works", scale it, and discover later that most of it was your own customers finding you

**Do this instead:** run Search for 4–6 weeks. Get clean conversion tracking. Learn what your real cost per customer is. *Then* add PMax and compare it against a known baseline.

## 📖 Real-World Example: Sana's Channel Ladder Over 12 Months

Sana's water filter store started with nothing. Here is exactly what she added, and when.

**Month 1–2: Brand Search only.** Budget PKR 15,000/month. Cost per sale: PKR 320. Almost pure profit, but only 40 sales a month. This paid for the next step.

**Month 3–4: High-intent Search.** Keywords like "water filter price karachi", "water purifier home delivery". Budget rose to PKR 80,000. Cost per sale: PKR 1,900. Above her PKR 2,600 margin? No — profitable, but thin. This is when she rebuilt the landing page (Lesson 0.5) and cost per sale dropped to PKR 1,667.

**Month 5: Standard Shopping.** She spent two weeks fixing her feed first: real titles, correct prices, good images. Cost per sale: PKR 1,450. Shopping quickly became her best channel.

**Month 6: Remarketing.** Small budget, PKR 10,000. Reminders to people who viewed a product but did not buy. Cost per sale: PKR 900.

**Month 8: Performance Max, with brand excluded.** She now had 120 conversions a month — Level 3. She launched PMax with a proper brand exclusion list. Cost per sale: PKR 1,600, and total sales grew 45%. Without the brand exclusion, she would have thought it was even better, and she would have been wrong.

**Month 11: Demand Gen.** Only after Search impression share hit 78% and further Search spending stopped producing more sales. She spent three weeks making four short videos before spending one rupee on media.

**What she never did:** she never ran cold Display targeting, and she never ran YouTube, because her cash could not survive a 12-week measurement window. Both were correct decisions for her stage.

**Her result:** 40 sales/month → 310 sales/month in one year, with POAS staying above 1.4 the whole time.

The order mattered more than the skill.

## 📌 Key Takeaways

- Capture existing demand before you try to create new demand.
- Search first, always. It is the fastest, clearest signal of whether the business works.
- Match your channel to your data level. Automation needs conversions to learn from.
- Never launch PMax as your first campaign. It hides the truth when you most need it.
- Add channels in order, and each one should be profitable before you add the next.

## 🎯 Expert Challenge

Build a 12-month channel roadmap for a real business.

- Define the trigger for each new channel. Not a date — a condition. For example: "Add PMax when we reach 60 conversions/month and brand exclusions are approved."
- Set a budget split for each phase, and state what percentage stays in proven channels versus experiments. A common split is 70% proven, 20% scaling, 10% testing.
- For each channel, write the measurement window: how long before you are allowed to judge it.
- Write the exit rule for each channel: the specific number at which you would pause it.
- Finally, find the ceiling. At what monthly spend does Search alone stop growing? Estimate it from impression share data, and explain what you would do when you hit it.
