## The Three Match Types

### Exact match — `[water filter karachi]`

Written in square brackets. Triggers on that search **and close variants**.

⚠️ **Important:** exact match has not been truly exact for years. It includes:
- Plurals and misspellings: "water filters karachi"
- Reordered words where meaning is unchanged: "karachi water filter"
- Words with the same meaning: "water purifier karachi"
- Implied words: "water filter in karachi"

**So "exact" now means "same meaning", not "same words".**

### Phrase match — `"water filter karachi"`

Written in quotation marks. Triggers when the **meaning of your phrase** is included in the search, with extra words allowed before or after.

- ✅ "best water filter karachi"
- ✅ "water filter karachi home delivery"
- ✅ "cheap water purifier in karachi"
- ❌ "water filter lahore" (meaning changed)

### Broad match — `water filter karachi`

No punctuation. Triggers on anything Google believes is related to the meaning and intent.

- ✅ "RO plant for home karachi"
- ✅ "clean drinking water solution karachi"
- ✅ "which water purifier is best"
- ⚠️ "water tanker service karachi" (related, but wrong)

## Why the Old Advice Is Now Wrong

If you read a guide written before about 2021, it says: *"Never use broad match. Use exact match only. Control everything."*

That advice was correct **when you set the bids**. If you manually bid PKR 40 on a keyword, broad match meant paying PKR 40 for any loosely related search. That was really dangerous.

**What changed:**

1. **Smart Bidding sets a different bid for every auction.** A poor-quality broad match search now gets a low bid automatically — or no bid at all.
2. **Google's language understanding improved dramatically.** Broad match in 2026 is far more accurate than in 2015.
3. **Search behaviour changed.** People type longer, more conversational queries (Lesson 1.1). Exact match lists cannot cover the variety.
4. **Over 15% of daily searches are brand new** — never searched before. No keyword list can contain them.

**Think of two ways to hire a receptionist.** The old way: give them a written list of exactly which callers to let through. Anyone not on the list is turned away, including your best customer with a new phone number. The new way: hire someone who understands your business and can judge each caller.

The second only works if the person is really capable, and if you tell them who to turn away. That is exactly the situation with broad match and Smart Bidding.

## The Four Conditions Broad Match Needs

Broad match is powerful **only** when all four of these are true. Missing any one of them turns it into a money burner.

### ✅ Condition 1: Accurate conversion tracking

Smart Bidding steers broad match. Smart Bidding learns from conversions. If your tracking is broken or measures the wrong thing, broad match will confidently find you the wrong customers.

*This is why Module 2 came before Module 3.*

### ✅ Condition 2: Smart Bidding, not manual bidding

Broad match with manual CPC is the 2015 disaster all over again. If you are on Manual CPC or Maximise Clicks, **do not use broad match**.

### ✅ Condition 3: Enough conversion volume

The AI needs data. Below roughly 15–30 conversions per month per campaign, broad match has too little signal to steer with.

### ✅ Condition 4: A strong negative keyword system

This is the steering wheel. Without it, broad match drifts. Lesson 3.4 builds this.

## The Control Layers

Broad match is not "letting Google do whatever it wants". You control it through five layers.

### Layer 1: Negative keywords
Your primary control. Both campaign-level and shared lists. Reviewed weekly.

### Layer 2: The bid strategy target
A tighter tCPA or higher tROAS target automatically restricts broad match to safer auctions. **This is the most underused control in Google Ads.** Do not want broad match wandering? Tighten the target before you touch anything else.

### Layer 3: Audience signals and observation
Adding audiences as "Observation" lets you see and adjust performance for specific groups without limiting reach.

### Layer 4: Campaign structure
Keeping broad match in its own campaign, with its own budget, contains any damage and makes results readable.

### Layer 5: Location, language and schedule
Basic settings that eliminate whole categories of irrelevant traffic before they cost anything.

## The Match Type Strategy by Maturity

Use the maturity levels from Lesson 1.2.

### Level 1: Under 15 conversions/month

**Use: Exact and Phrase only.**

You cannot afford exploration. Every rupee must go to a search you are confident about. Broad match here will spend your small budget learning things you cannot afford to learn.

- Start with exact match on your 10–20 best keywords
- Add phrase match for your main themes
- Build negatives from day one

### Level 2: 15–50 conversions/month

**Use: Phrase primarily, with a controlled broad match test.**

- Keep your proven exact and phrase keywords running
- Create **one separate campaign** for broad match, with 15–20% of your budget
- Use Maximise Conversions with a tCPA
- Review search terms twice a week, not weekly

### Level 3: 50–200 conversions/month

**Use: Broad match as a core part of the account.**

- Broad match in main campaigns, with Smart Bidding and tight targets
- Keep exact match for your top proven converters — it gives predictable volume
- Weekly search term mining
- Shared negative lists across campaigns

### Level 4: 200+ conversions/month

**Use: Broad-led, with everything else supporting.**

- Broad match plus value-based bidding
- Exact match retained for brand and top performers
- N-gram analysis monthly (Lesson 3.4)
- Broad match now discovers demand you would never have found

## How to Test Broad Match Safely

Never convert an entire account to broad match at once. Use this process.

**Step 1.** Create a new campaign. Name it clearly: `Search | PK-KHI | Water Filters | Broad | Sales`.

**Step 2.** Copy your best-performing ad groups into it, converting keywords to broad match.

**Step 3.** Set the budget to **15–20% of the parent campaign**. Not more.

**Step 4.** Apply your existing negative keyword lists immediately, before it goes live.

**Step 5.** Use the same bid strategy and the same target as the proven campaign. This makes the comparison fair.

**Step 6.** Review search terms every 2–3 days for the first two weeks. Add negatives aggressively.

**Step 7.** Judge after 4 weeks, on cost per *conversion* and conversion volume — not on cost per click. Broad match often has a higher CPC and a better cost per conversion at the same time.

**Step 8.** If it works, gradually increase its budget. If it does not, check the four conditions before concluding broad match "does not work".

---

## Should You Still Use Exact Match at All?

Yes. It is not obsolete. Keep exact match for:

1. **Brand terms.** You want tight control over your own name.
2. **Your top 10–20 proven converters.** Predictable volume, predictable cost.
3. **Legally sensitive or high-risk terms** where you cannot afford drift.
4. **Very small budgets** where every impression matters.

**What has changed** is that exact match is no longer the *default*. It is now a specialist tool for specific jobs.

## 📖 Real-World Example: TaskFlow's Broad Match Journey — Both Failures and Success

TaskFlow tried broad match three times. The first two failed. Understanding why is more useful than the success story.

### Attempt 1 (Month 4): Complete failure

They switched their whole account to broad match after reading an article.

**Their situation at the time:**
- 11 conversions/month (Level 1)
- Conversion action: free trial signup (the wrong thing to count — Lesson 0.6)
- Bid strategy: Maximise Clicks
- Negative keywords: 4

**Result in 3 weeks:** spend up 240%, signups up 180%, paying customers **down**. Search terms included "free project management software", "project management jobs", "asana tutorial", "gantt chart excel template".

**What went wrong:** all four conditions failed at once. Wrong conversion action, no Smart Bidding, not enough volume, almost no negatives.

They concluded "broad match does not work". That conclusion was wrong, but understandable.

### Attempt 2 (Month 9): Partial failure

They tried again after fixing conversion tracking. Now counting PQLs (Lesson 0.6).

**Their situation:**
- 34 conversions/month (Level 2) ✅
- Correct conversion action ✅
- Maximise Conversions ✅
- Negative list: 60 terms ✅

**But** they switched **all** campaigns to broad match at once, with no budget containment.

**Result:** cost per customer rose from $198 to $260 over 6 weeks. Not a disaster, but worse. And because everything changed at once, they could not tell which part failed.

**What went wrong:** the strategy was right; the *rollout* was wrong. No control campaign, no comparison, no containment.

### Attempt 3 (Month 14): Success

**Their situation:**
- 71 conversions/month (Level 3)
- Offline conversion imports live (Lesson 2.6 paying off)
- tCPA bidding with a target based on real customer value
- Negative list: 310 terms, reviewed weekly

**What they did differently:**
- One new broad campaign only, at 18% of budget
- Existing campaigns untouched as a control
- Search term review every 2 days for 3 weeks
- 4-week judgement window agreed in advance

**Results after 8 weeks:**

| | Exact/Phrase campaigns | Broad campaign |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per click | $3.40 | $4.10 |
| Conversion rate | 4.2% | 3.6% |
| **Cost per paying customer** | **$198** | **$164** |
| New keyword themes discovered | 0 | 11 |

Note that **CPC was higher and conversion rate was lower** — yet cost per customer was better. Broad match was finding a different, better kind of buyer that their keyword list had never covered.

Those 11 discovered themes were the real prize. Terms like "software for construction site progress tracking" — phrases nobody would ever have written into a keyword list.

They moved broad to 45% of budget over the next quarter.

**The lesson across all three attempts:** broad match is not good or bad. It is a tool that needs four conditions and a controlled rollout. Most people who say it failed had at least one condition missing.

## 📌 Key Takeaways

- Exact match now means "same meaning", not "same words". Nothing is truly exact any more.
- Broad match became powerful because Smart Bidding sets a different bid for every auction.
- Broad match needs four conditions: accurate tracking, Smart Bidding, enough volume, strong negatives.
- Your bid strategy target is a control layer. Tighten the target to restrict broad match reach.
- Test broad match in a separate, budget-capped campaign, with an untouched control to compare against.

## 🎯 Expert Challenge

Design a full match type migration plan for a mature account.

- Map every current keyword by match type, spend and performance.
- Find which exact match keywords are earning their place, and which are simply duplicating what broad would find anyway.
- Design the phased migration: which campaigns move first, what budget percentage, what stays as a control, and over how many weeks.
- Define the negative keyword preparation needed before each phase, including the n-gram analysis from Lesson 3.4.
- Set the decision rules in advance: the exact numbers at which you expand, hold or roll back. Write them down before launch so you cannot rationalise later.
- Finally, model the risk: what is the maximum money you could lose if the migration goes badly, and is that acceptable to the business? If you cannot answer this, your test is too big.
