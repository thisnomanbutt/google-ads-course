## Why This Lesson Exists

Google Ads has hundreds of screens. Most courses skip straight to strategy and leave beginners quietly lost.

**You cannot follow "go to the search terms report" if you do not know where that is.**

This lesson is your map. It is short on theory and long on where things live.

⚠️ **Google moves things around.** Menu names and positions change every year. The *ideas* below stay the same even when the layout shifts. If a menu is not where this lesson says, use the search box at the top — it finds any screen by name.

## The Five Parts of the Screen

Every Google Ads screen has the same five areas.

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  1. TOP BAR — account, search, help, settings    │
├───────────┬──────────────────────────────────────┤
│           │  3. DATE RANGE (top right)           │
│  2. LEFT  ├──────────────────────────────────────┤
│  MENU     │  4. TABLE — your campaigns, keywords │
│           │                                      │
│           │  5. COLUMNS + FILTERS above table    │
└───────────┴──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 1. The top bar

Holds your account name, a search box, and settings.

**The search box is the most useful thing on the screen.** Type "search terms" or "negative keywords" and it takes you there. Use it instead of hunting through menus.

### 2. The left menu

This is how you move between sections. The main ones:

| Menu | What is inside |
|---|---|
| **Campaigns** | Your campaigns, ad groups, ads, keywords — where you spend most of your time |
| **Goals** | Conversions — what counts as success (Lesson 2.3) |
| **Audiences** | Groups of people you can target or watch (Lesson 6.7) |
| **Tools** | Keyword Planner, change history, shared lists, scripts |
| **Billing** | Payment method and invoices |
| **Admin** | Account access and settings |

### 3. The date range

Top right. **Everything on the screen obeys this.**

⚠️ **This causes more beginner confusion than anything else.** You look at a campaign, see zero conversions, and panic — but the date range was set to "Today" and it is 9am.

**Always check the date range before you believe a number.**

### 4. The table

The main area. What it shows depends on where you are in the left menu.

### 5. Columns and filters

Above the table. **Columns** decide which numbers you see. **Filters** decide which rows you see.

Lesson 1.3 covers setting these up properly. For now, just know they exist.

## Moving Between the Four Layers

From Lesson 1.0, an account has four layers. Here is how you move through them.

**To go down a layer:** click the name.

```
Campaigns  →  click "Search — Lahore — Implants"
           →  now you are inside that campaign
           →  click "Ad groups" in the left menu
           →  click "Dental implants"
           →  now you are inside that ad group
```

**To go back up:** use the breadcrumb trail at the top, or click "All campaigns".

⚠️ **A very common beginner mistake.** You go into one campaign, then wonder why the other campaigns disappeared. You have not lost them. You are inside one campaign, and the screen is only showing that campaign's contents.

**Look at the top of the screen.** It always tells you where you are.

## The Screens You Will Actually Use

Out of hundreds of screens, these are the ones that matter. Learn where these are and you can do 90% of the work.

| Screen | Where | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| **Campaigns** | Campaigns → Campaigns | Budgets, status, overall numbers |
| **Ad groups** | Campaigns → Ad groups | Theme-level performance |
| **Keywords** | Campaigns → Audiences, keywords and content → Search keywords | What you are bidding on |
| **Search terms** | Same section → Search terms | ⭐ **What people actually typed** |
| **Negative keywords** | Same section → Negative search keywords | Words you are blocking |
| **Ads** | Campaigns → Ads | Your actual ad text |
| **Assets** | Campaigns → Assets | Sitelinks, callouts, images (Lesson 4.2) |
| **Conversions** | Goals → Conversions → Summary | What counts as success |
| **Change history** | Tools → Change history | Who changed what, and when |
| **Keyword Planner** | Tools → Planning → Keyword Planner | Finding new keywords |

### The one to learn first

⭐ **The search terms report.**

Keywords are what you *bid on*. Search terms are what people *actually typed*. They are not the same, and the difference is where your money leaks.

**You will open this screen every week for the rest of your career.** Lesson 3.4 is entirely about how to use it.

## Reading the Numbers on Screen

You will see these columns constantly. Here is what each one means.

| Column | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| **Impressions (Impr.)** | How many times your ad was shown |
| **Clicks** | How many people clicked it |
| **CTR** | Clicks ÷ impressions. The share of people who clicked. |
| **Avg. CPC** | The average you paid per click |
| **Cost** | Total money spent |
| **Conversions** | How many times someone did what you wanted |
| **Cost / conv.** | Cost ÷ conversions. What each result cost you. |
| **Conv. rate** | Conversions ÷ clicks. The share of clickers who converted. |
| **Conv. value** | The money value of those conversions, if you set values |

### The two that matter most

**Cost per conversion** and **conversion value**. Everything else is supporting detail.

⚠️ **Impressions and CTR feel important and mostly are not.** A high CTR on traffic that never buys is an expensive way to feel good. Lesson 1.3 sets up columns that show profit instead.

## Buttons to Be Careful With

A few things are easy to press by accident.

| Button | What it does | Care level |
|---|---|---|
| **Pause / Enable** toggle | Stops or starts a campaign | 🟡 Easy to hit the wrong row |
| **Remove** | Deletes a keyword or ad | 🔴 Some removals cannot be undone |
| **Apply all recommendations** | Makes many changes at once | 🔴 **Never press this** |
| **Budget field** | Changes daily spend | 🟡 Typing an extra zero is a real risk |
| **Bid strategy** | Changes how Google bids | 🔴 Restarts learning (Lesson 5.2) |

### The one rule

⚠️ **Never press "Apply all" on Google's recommendations.**

Google suggests changes that often increase your spend. Some are good; many are not right for your business. Lesson 1.3 shows you how to switch auto-apply off, and Lesson 10.4 explains why Google's suggestions and your profit are not always the same thing.

**Read each recommendation. Apply the ones you understand. Dismiss the rest.**

## If You Get Lost

Three ways back.

1. **Click the Google Ads logo** at the top left — returns you to the account overview
2. **Use the search box** at the top — type what you want and go straight there
3. **Tools → Change history** — if something looks wrong, this shows what changed and when

**Change history is your safety net.** It records every change, who made it, and the old value. If a number suddenly looks wrong, look here first.

## 📖 Real-World Example: The Panic That Was Not a Problem

Dr. Ayesha had been running ads for three weeks. Bilal had set everything up and she was watching it herself.

On a Tuesday morning she messaged him: **"Everything has stopped. Zero conversions. Zero clicks. Did we get banned?"**

### What had actually happened

She had clicked the date range and selected **"Today"**.

It was 8:40am. Her clinic's ads ran from 9am. **Nothing had happened yet because the day had not started.**

Her campaigns were completely fine. The previous week had been her best ever.

### The second false alarm

Two weeks later, another message: **"My implant campaign has disappeared."**

She had clicked into her *general dentistry* campaign, and was looking at its ad groups. The implant campaign was still there — she was just inside a different campaign and the screen was only showing that one.

### What Bilal did

He did not explain it again over messages. He sent her a short checklist and told her to run it before reporting any problem.

> **Before you panic, check these three:**
> 1. **What is the date range?** Set it to "Last 7 days" and look again.
> 2. **Where am I?** Look at the top of the screen. Am I inside one campaign?
> 3. **What does change history say?** Tools → Change history. Did anyone change anything?

**She never sent another false alarm.**

### Why this matters more than it looks

Those two messages cost Bilal about forty minutes. Spread over many clients, that is real time.

But the bigger cost was hers. **For twenty minutes she believed her business had been shut down.** That is a horrible feeling, and it was caused entirely by not knowing where she was on the screen.

⚠️ **Most beginner "emergencies" are one of those three things.** Learn the map and you stop having them.

## 📌 Key Takeaways

## 💪 Practical Exercise (30 minutes)

Learn the map by walking it. Use any account you can view, even a demo one.

1. Find the **search terms report**. Write down the path you took.
2. Find **change history**. Write down the last three changes you see.
3. Find where **negative keywords** live.
4. Find where **conversions** are set up.
5. Change the date range to "Last 30 days", then "Today". Notice how every number changes.
6. Click into one campaign, then find your way back to seeing all campaigns.
7. Use the top search box to reach "Keyword Planner" without using the menu.

Do this until you can find all five screens without thinking. **It takes about twenty minutes and saves you hours later.**
