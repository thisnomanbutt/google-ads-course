## Part 1: Getting Paid in USD

You have four realistic routes. Most successful freelancers use two at once.

### Route 1: Payoneer

The most common starting point for Pakistani freelancers.

- Client pays into your Payoneer receiving account
- You withdraw to your Pakistani bank in PKR, or use the Payoneer card
- Widely accepted by Upwork, Fiverr and many direct clients

**Good for:** beginners, small amounts, first international clients.
**Watch out for:** withdrawal fees and the exchange rate margin. Compare the rate you actually receive, not the rate advertised.

### Route 2: Direct bank transfer to a Pakistani bank

Your bank receives the USD wire and converts it.

- Fully legal and fully documented
- Creates the proof of export earnings you need for tax benefits
- Some banks are slow and ask many questions

**Good for:** larger, regular payments from serious clients.
**Watch out for:** wire fees on both sides, and delays if your paperwork is unclear. Ask your bank for a written list of needed documents before the first payment.

### Route 3: A foreign company (usually a US LLC) with a business account

This is the route many agency owners eventually take. Read Part 2 carefully before choosing it.

### Route 4: Platform payouts (Upwork, Fiverr)

Slower and more expensive, but simple and safe when you are starting.

### One rule that applies to all four routes

**Bring the money into Pakistan through official banking channels.**

Using informal money transfer to avoid paperwork will:
- Make you unable to prove your income
- Block you from tax benefits for IT exports
- Create serious legal risk
- Make it impossible to grow into a real company

Document everything. Boring is safe.

## Part 2: Do You Actually Need a US LLC?

Many YouTubers say "form an LLC" as if it is free. It is not. Let's be honest about both sides.

### What an LLC really gives you

- A US business bank account, which opens more payment tools
- A business card that works reliably for Google Ads billing
- More trust from US and European clients
- Cleaner contracts and invoices
- Separation between your personal money and business money

### What an LLC actually costs you

- State formation fees, plus a yearly renewal fee
- A registered agent, paid every year
- **US tax filings, even with zero income.** A single-member LLC owned by a non-US person generally must file specific IRS forms every year. Missing them can carry very large penalties.
- Bookkeeping, and usually a US accountant
- Time and paperwork you did not have before

### The honest decision rule

| Your situation | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Earning under about $1,500/month | Do not form an LLC yet. Use Payoneer. |
| Earning $1,500–4,000/month, mostly platforms | Probably still not worth it |
| Earning $4,000+/month from direct Western clients | Now it usually makes sense |
| Clients refusing to pay a person, only a company | It makes sense earlier |
| You want to look impressive | Bad reason. Skip it. |

⚠️ **The trap:** people form an LLC, ignore the annual filings for two years, then discover the penalties. If you cannot commit to the yearly paperwork and its cost, do not form the company.

### If you do form one

1. Speak to an accountant who has actually handled non-resident-owned US companies. Not a general one.
2. Understand your filing calendar *before* you register. Write the dates down.
3. Budget for annual costs, not just setup costs.
4. Keep business and personal money completely separate. Mixing them removes the protection you paid for.
5. Remember you still have Pakistani tax obligations. A foreign company does not make you invisible to your own country's tax authority.

### Also do the local basics

- Register your business in Pakistan properly (sole proprietor is a fine start)
- Look into registration with the relevant IT export body, which can unlock reduced tax rates for software and IT-enabled service exports
- Get an NTN and file your returns
- Keep every invoice and bank advice slip

Being properly registered locally is cheaper, faster and more useful than a foreign company for most people.

## Part 3: Client Contracts and Pricing in PKR

### The currency problem

The rupee moves. If you quote PKR 150,000 per month for a year, and the rupee weakens, you have quietly given yourself a pay cut.

**Three ways to protect yourself:**

1. **Price in USD, collect in PKR at the spot rate.** State it clearly: "Fee: USD 500, payable in PKR at the exchange rate on the invoice date."
2. **Add a review clause.** "Fees are reviewed if the USD/PKR rate moves more than 7% from the rate at signing."
3. **Bill quarterly instead of yearly.** Shorter commitments carry less currency risk.

### Ad spend must never touch your money

This is the most important rule in this whole lesson.

**Never pay for a client's ads with your own card and get reimbursed later.**

If the client delays payment by 30 days, you are financing their business with money you do not have. Many small agencies have died this way.

Instead:

- The client's own card is on the account. Always the first choice.
- Or the client pre-pays the ad budget into your account **before** the month starts.
- Never start spending on credit you cannot afford to lose.

### What every contract must contain

Even a simple two-page agreement should say:

- **Scope:** exactly what you do, and what you do not do
- **Fee, currency and payment date**
- **Who pays Google, and how**
- **Account ownership:** the client owns the Google Ads account and the data. Say this clearly. It builds trust and prevents ugly arguments later.
- **Notice period:** 30 days on both sides
- **What happens if the client stops paying:** you pause work after X days
- **No guarantee of specific results.** You guarantee process and effort, never a specific ROAS. This protects you.
- **Approval for spend changes** above an agreed limit

Get it signed. Email agreement is better than nothing, but a signed PDF is better.

## Part 4: Google Ads Billing Traps

### The card problem

Pakistani debit cards often fail on Google Ads, especially for USD accounts. Common causes:

- International transactions not enabled on the card
- Bank blocking recurring foreign charges
- Monthly foreign transaction limits
- 3D Secure not supported for the recurring charge

**What to do, in order:**

1. Call your bank and clearly enable international *and recurring* online transactions.
2. Ask what the monthly foreign transaction limit is. Raise it if needed.
3. If the card still fails, try a card from a different bank.
4. Consider a Payoneer card or a business card from your LLC account.
5. As a backup, ask your client to put their own card on the account. In most cases this is the correct setup anyway.

### Currency and timezone: choose once, forever

We cover this fully in Lesson 2.1, but the warning belongs here too.

**When you create a Google Ads account, you choose the currency and timezone. You cannot change them later.** You would have to build a new account and lose all history.

Simple rules:
- Working with local Pakistani clients → PKR and Pakistan Standard Time
- Working with US clients → their currency and their business timezone
- Never pick a timezone "because it is easier for me". Pick the one matching the client's business day, so daily budgets reset at the right moment.

### Watch out for VAT and taxes on ad spend

Google may add local taxes to your ad invoices depending on your billing country. Your PKR 100,000 budget may cost more than PKR 100,000 out of your bank.

Check the invoice, not the dashboard. Include this in your budget planning, and tell the client in advance.

## Part 5: Getting Google Support With No Local Office

There is no walk-in Google office to visit. But support does exist. Here is how to actually use it.

### The support ladder

**Level 1: Help Centre and chat support**
Available inside the Google Ads interface. Fine for billing errors and simple account questions. Weak for complex policy problems.

**Level 2: Written appeals**
For policy and suspension issues, the appeal form is the correct route — not chat. Chat agents usually cannot overturn a policy decision.

**Level 3: The Google Ads Community forums**
Product Experts there are experienced advertisers, not Google staff, but some have direct escalation paths. A clearly written post with real details gets real help.

**Level 4: A Google account representative**
Reps are usually assigned based on spend and growth potential, and coverage varies by market. Managing several accounts under one MCC (manager account) can make you visible to the partner team. Full strategy for this is in Lesson 10.4.

### How to write a support message that works

Most Pakistani freelancers get poor support because of *how* they ask, not where they are.

Use this structure:

1. **Account ID** in the first line. Always.
2. **One sentence describing the problem.** No story.
3. **What you already tried**, listed.
4. **What you want**, specifically.
5. **Evidence**, attached or linked.

**Weak message:** "My account is suspended please help me sir it is very urgent my client is angry."

**Strong message:** "Account 123-456-7890 was suspended on 12 September for Misrepresentation. Our landing page shows full business details, refund policy and contact information at [URL]. We have removed the claim 'guaranteed results' from all ads and pages. Requesting a review of the suspension. Screenshots attached."

The second one gets solved. The first one gets a copy-paste reply.

### Two hard truths

- **Being polite and precise beats being urgent.** Emotional messages get slower responses.
- **Never use fake information to get support or to open accounts.** Google detects it, and account bans for circumvention are extremely hard to reverse. See Lesson 1.5.

## 📖 Real-World Example: Bilal's First Year

Bilal from Rawalpindi started in 2024 with one Fiverr client at $150 a month.

**Months 1–4.** He used Payoneer only. Fees were high but everything worked. He kept every invoice in one folder. Total earnings: about $900.

**Month 5.** A US client offered $1,200 a month but wanted to pay a company, not a person. Bilal almost rushed into forming an LLC. Instead he asked the client if a signed contract with a registered Pakistani sole proprietorship would work. It did. Cost: a few thousand rupees and one week.

**Month 7.** He made his one serious mistake. A client asked him to "just put the ad spend on your card, we will pay next month." He spent $2,300 of his own money. The client paid 68 days later. He nearly could not pay his own rent.

He added one line to his contract that week: *"Client's payment method must be on the Google Ads account. The agency does not fund ad spend."* He has never broken that rule since.

**Month 9.** He registered with the relevant IT export scheme and started filing properly with an accountant. This cost him money and gave him something more valuable: he could now prove his income, which later helped when he applied for a car loan.

**Month 14.** At around $5,000 monthly revenue from three direct US clients, he formed a US LLC — with an accountant who explained the annual filing what you need before he signed anything. He put the annual deadlines in his calendar with a two-month warning.

**Month 18.** One client account was suspended for misrepresentation. Because he had documentation, a real registered business and a clean history, his written appeal succeeded in 6 days.

Bilal's advice, in his own words: *"Get boring first. Get impressive later."*

## 📌 Key Takeaways

- Bring every rupee of foreign income through official banking channels. Documentation is protection.
- Do not form a US LLC until the money justifies the annual cost and paperwork.
- Price in USD, collect in PKR, and never fund a client's ad spend with your own money.
- Currency and timezone are permanent choices on a Google Ads account. Get them right on day one.
- Support responds to precision, not urgency. Lead with the account ID and the facts.

## 💪 Practical Exercise (30 minutes)

Build your business foundation checklist.

1. Write down every way you currently receive money, with the real fee percentage for each.
2. Call your bank and ask two questions: is international recurring payment enabled, and what is my monthly limit? Write the answers.
3. Open your current client agreement. Find the line about who pays Google. If it is missing, write it now.
4. Draft a suspension appeal template with blank spaces for account ID, date and reason. Save it.
5. Put one recurring reminder in your calendar: "Check tax and banking rules" every 6 months.

## 🎯 Expert Challenge

Build a complete operating model for a 5-client agency.

- Map the money flow: client → your account → Google → your profit. Include every fee and tax at each step.
- Calculate your true margin per client after payment fees, currency conversion and taxes. Most people discover it is 10–15% lower than they thought.
- Write the decision rule for when you would form a foreign company, with the specific monthly revenue number and the annual cost you would accept.
- Write a one-page client onboarding pack: contract terms, billing setup steps, account access instructions, and what the client is responsible for.
- Finally, write your risk plan: what you do if a client stops paying, if a card fails mid-month, or if an account is suspended on a Friday night.
