## Why Rescues Are Different From Builds

When you build from scratch (Lesson 4.6), everything is your decision and nothing is hiding.

When you inherit an account, three things are true:

1. **Someone else's decisions are everywhere**, and you do not know why they made them
2. **The data may be lying to you**, so you cannot trust what you see
3. **Somebody is emotionally attached** to what is there

⚠️ **The biggest rescue mistake is starting to optimise immediately.** You see something obviously wrong, fix it, then something else, then something else — and three weeks later you have changed forty things, nothing has settled, and you cannot tell what helped.

> **A rescue is diagnosis first, then foundations, then patience, then improvement. In that order, always.**

---

## The Golden Rule of Week One

**Change almost nothing.**

You are allowed to do exactly two things in week one:

1. **Stop obvious bleeding** — pause anything clearly and badly losing money
2. **Find out what is true**

That is all. Everything else waits.

⚠️ **Why:** if you change ten things at once and results improve, you have learned nothing about which change worked. And if results get worse, you cannot undo the right one.

---

## The First Hour: Before You Touch Anything

Do these in this order. It takes about an hour.

### 1. Check the tracking is honest (20 minutes)

**Never trust the numbers until you have checked this.**

- Compare Google Ads conversions to the business's own records. Do they roughly match?
- How many Primary conversion actions are there? (More than 2 is a problem — Lesson 2.3)
- Is anything counted twice? (Lesson 2.4)
- Complete a real conversion yourself and watch it arrive (Lesson 2.5)

⚠️ **If Google says 400 conversions and the business made 90 sales, stop.** Everything else you would do is based on false data. Fixing tracking is the whole job until it is fixed.

### 2. Read the change history (10 minutes)

Tools → Change history. Look at the last 90 days.

**You are looking for:** what changed just before performance dropped, and whether anyone is making changes every few days (which prevents learning — Lesson 5.2).

### 3. Look at where the money goes (15 minutes)

Sort campaigns by cost, highest first. Then sort search terms by cost.

**Do not judge yet. Just look.** You are building a picture.

### 4. Check the settings that quietly waste money (10 minutes)

From Lesson 4.4:

- Is location set to **Presence**, or "Presence or interest"?
- Is the **Display Network** switched on inside Search campaigns?
- Are **auto-apply recommendations** on?

### 5. Get the business numbers (5 minutes)

If you do not have them: margin, close rate, what a customer is worth. **Without these you cannot say whether anything is working** (Lesson 0.3).

---

## The Order to Fix Things

This order is not a preference. Doing it differently wastes the work.

```
WEEK 1   Diagnose. Stop obvious bleeding. Nothing else.
   ↓
WEEK 2-3 Fix tracking. Fix what counts as a conversion.
   ↓        Fix obvious waste: locations, negatives, loss-makers.
   ↓
WEEK 4-5 Fix structure and bidding IF genuinely broken.
   ↓
WEEK 6-8 STOP. Change nothing for 21 days.
   ↓
WEEK 9+  Now improve: Quality Score, landing pages, scaling.
```

### Why tracking comes first

Everything downstream depends on it. Smart Bidding learns from your conversion data. **If that data is wrong, better bidding just finds the wrong thing faster.**

### Why "stop changing things" is a step

⚠️ **Week 6–8 is the hardest part of any rescue, and the part people skip.**

Everyone wants to see activity. Doing nothing for three weeks feels like not working.

**But Smart Bidding cannot settle while you keep changing things.** The stability *is* the intervention. Lesson 5.2 explains the mechanism.

---

## Telling the Client What Is Coming

This conversation decides whether your rescue survives.

### The thing you must say, in writing, before you start

> "The first 30 days are diagnosis and foundations. **Reported numbers will probably get worse during this period, and that is expected.** Here is why: the current tracking is counting things that are not real sales. When I fix it, the conversion number will fall — not because we are doing worse, but because we will finally be counting honestly. Real improvement shows from around day 60."

⚠️ **If you do not say this in advance, you will be fired in week three.**

The numbers genuinely do get worse first. A client who was not warned sees falling conversions and concludes you broke it. A client who was warned sees the prediction come true and trusts you more.

### Keep a weekly note

During a rescue, send a short written update every Friday. Three lines:

- What I did this week
- What I found
- What happens next week

**Silence during a period of falling numbers is how rescues get cancelled.**

## 📖 Real-World Example: A Live Rescue, Week by Week

A Lahore furniture retailer. PKR 340,000 monthly ad spend. Two agencies in eighteen months.

**The owner's brief:** "Sales are down 40% but we spend the same. Fix it fast."

### Week 1 — Diagnosis only

Bilal changed almost nothing. He spent four hours looking.

**What the tracking check found:**

| | Google Ads says | Business records say |
|---|---|---|
| Conversions last month | 412 | — |
| Actual orders | — | 96 |

**A 4x gap.** The account was counting:

- Every "Add to cart" as a conversion
- Every newsletter signup as a conversion
- Every WhatsApp button click as a conversion
- And purchases were being counted twice (a hardcoded tag plus a GTM tag, no transaction ID — Lesson 2.4)

**Six Primary conversion actions.** Google had been chasing all six equally.

**What change history showed:** the previous agency had changed bid strategy 14 times in 90 days. **Nothing had ever completed a learning phase.**

**What settings showed:**
- Location set to "Presence or interest" — 22% of spend was outside Pakistan
- Display Network switched on inside two Search campaigns

**One thing he did change:** he paused a campaign spending PKR 2,100/day on "furniture design ideas" keywords with zero sales in four months. That was the obvious bleeding.

**He wrote to the client:**

> "I have found the main problem. Your conversion tracking is counting cart additions and newsletter signups as sales, and counting real sales twice. Your true cost per sale is around PKR 3,540, not the PKR 825 your reports show. When I fix this next week, your reported conversions will fall by roughly 75%. **This is the number becoming honest, not performance getting worse.** Expect real improvement from day 60."

### Weeks 2–3 — Foundations

**Tracking rebuilt:**
- One Primary conversion: Purchase
- Everything else moved to Secondary (still reported, not chased)
- Transaction ID added, so double counting stopped
- Real order value sent as the conversion value
- Tested with three live orders (Lesson 2.5)

**Reported conversions fell from 412 to 98.** As predicted.

**Waste removed:**
- Location changed to **Presence** → recovered about PKR 74,000/month
- Display Network switched off in Search campaigns
- Auto-apply turned off
- Ran an n-gram analysis (Lesson 3.4) and added 280 negative keywords — mostly `free`, `diy`, `how to make`, `second hand`, `repair`, `jobs`

### Weeks 4–5 — Structure and bidding

The account had **31 campaigns**, most with under 5 conversions a month. Classic fragmentation (Lesson 1.4).

He consolidated to **6 campaigns**, split only by real reasons: budget, location, product margin.

Bid strategy set to Maximise Conversion Value with a target derived from actual performance (Lesson 5.3), not from the client's hopes.

### Weeks 6–8 — The hard part

**He changed nothing for 21 days.**

The client messaged twice asking what was being done. Both times Bilal replied with the same thing: the campaigns are learning, and changing anything now restarts the clock. He sent the weekly note each Friday anyway.

### Weeks 9–16 — Improvement

Only now did he optimise:

- Quality Score work on the top 30 keywords (Lesson 4.5) — average moved from 4.4 to 7.8
- Two dedicated landing pages replacing the category-page destinations (Lesson 0.5)
- Budget raised on the two campaigns that were losing impressions to budget (Lesson 8.1)

### The numbers, month by month

| | Month 0 | Month 1 | Month 2 | Month 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spend | PKR 340,000 | PKR 322,000 | PKR 318,000 | PKR 341,000 |
| **Reported conversions** | **412** | **98** | 112 | 174 |
| **Actual orders** | **96** | 104 | 138 | **229** |
| Cost per real order | PKR 3,540 | PKR 3,096 | PKR 2,304 | **PKR 1,489** |
| Gross profit from ads | PKR 38,400 | PKR 104,000 | PKR 262,200 | PKR 596,800 |

**Read month 1 carefully.** Reported conversions fell 76%. Actual orders went *up*. That single row is the whole lesson.

### What the client said at month 4

> "In week three I nearly stopped this. The number on my dashboard had collapsed and it looked like a disaster. The only reason I didn't was that you had told me in writing, before you started, that exactly this would happen."

### What actually did the work

Ranked by contribution:

| Fix | Share of the improvement |
|---|---|
| Fixing what counted as a conversion | ~40% |
| Location setting (Presence) | ~20% |
| Consolidating 31 campaigns into 6 | ~15% |
| Letting it settle for 21 days | ~12% |
| Negative keywords | ~8% |
| Quality Score and landing pages | ~5% |

⚠️ **Notice what is at the bottom.** The clever optimisation work — the part that feels like skill — contributed least.

**The rescue was won in weeks 2 and 3, by fixing measurement and settings.** Everything after that was refinement.

## 📌 Key Takeaways

- Diagnose first, then foundations, then hold still, then improve. Order matters more than skill.
- In week one, change almost nothing. Stop obvious bleeding and find out what is true.
- Never trust the numbers until you have compared them to the business's own records.
- Warn the client in writing that reported numbers will fall first. Without that warning, you get fired in week three.
- Most rescues are won by fixing measurement and settings, not by clever optimisation.

## 💪 Practical Exercise (60 minutes)

Run the first hour on a real inherited account.

1. Compare Google Ads conversions to the business's own sales records for last month. Write down the gap.
2. Count the Primary conversion actions.
3. Open change history. List every change in the last 90 days, and count them.
4. Check the three settings: location option, Display Network in Search, auto-apply.
5. Sort search terms by cost. Write down the top five with zero conversions.
6. Write the message you would send the client before starting, including the warning about numbers falling.

## 🎯 Expert Challenge

Build a rescue playbook you can reuse.

- Turn the first hour into a checklist with the exact screens, in order, and a place to record findings. It should be usable by someone who has never seen the account.
- Build the diagnostic decision tree: if tracking is broken, do X; if structure is fragmented, do Y; if nothing is wrong technically but performance dropped, go to Lesson 8.4's eight causes.
- Write the client communication pack: the pre-start warning, the weekly note format, and the day-30 review. Make the pre-start warning specific enough that it predicts an actual number, as Bilal's did.
- Design the "what did the work" analysis: after any rescue, attribute the recovery across the changes you made. Most people never do this, so they keep believing the clever work mattered most.
- Then run it on a real account and time each phase. Compare your estimate of what would help against what actually did.

**That last comparison is the calibration exercise from Lesson 13.1, applied to yourself.** Most practitioners discover they systematically overrate the sophisticated fixes and underrate the boring ones.
