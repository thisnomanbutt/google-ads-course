## The Money Words

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Bid** | The most you agree to pay for one click. You usually pay less. |
| **CPC** | Cost per click. What one click actually cost you. |
| **Budget** | The most you want to spend per day on a campaign. |
| **Spend** / **Cost** | Money actually used. |
| **CPA** | Cost per acquisition. What one conversion cost you. Also called cost per conversion. |
| **ROAS** | Return on ad spend. Revenue ÷ ad spend. Spend PKR 1, get PKR 4 back = 4x. |
| **POAS** | Return on ad spend using **profit** instead of revenue. More honest. → Lesson 0.4 |
| **Margin** | What is left from a sale after the costs of delivering it. → Lesson 0.3 |
| **Breakeven ROAS** | The ROAS where you make no profit and no loss. → Lesson 0.3 |
| **LTV** | Lifetime value. Everything a customer is worth over time, not just the first sale. → Lesson 0.3 |
| **CAC** | Customer acquisition cost. What you pay to get one customer. |

### The one people get wrong

⚠️ **ROAS is not profit.** A 4x ROAS sounds excellent. If your margin is 20%, a 4x ROAS means you are **losing money**.

This is the single most expensive misunderstanding in advertising, and Lesson 0.4 is entirely about it.

## The Traffic Words

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Impression** | Your ad was shown once. Free — you only pay for clicks. |
| **Click** | Someone clicked your ad. This is what you pay for. |
| **CTR** | Click-through rate. Clicks ÷ impressions. |
| **Conversion** | The action you wanted: a sale, a booking, a call. |
| **Conversion rate** | Conversions ÷ clicks. |
| **Impression share** | The share of available auctions you actually appeared in. → Lesson 8.1 |
| **Auction** | The instant decision about which ads to show. Happens every search. → Lesson 1.1 |
| **Ad Rank** | The score deciding your position. Roughly bid × quality. → Lesson 1.1 |
| **Quality Score** | Google's 1–10 rating of how relevant you are. Higher = cheaper clicks. → Lesson 4.5 |

## The Words People Mix Up Most

These four pairs cause the most confusion. Learn these and you avoid most beginner errors.

### Keyword vs search term

| | |
|---|---|
| **Keyword** | What **you** chose to bid on |
| **Search term** | What the **person actually typed** |

You bid on `water filter`. Someone types `water filter repair near me`. Your ad can appear. **That is where money leaks**, and Lesson 3.4 is about plugging it.

### Campaign vs ad group

| | |
|---|---|
| **Campaign** | Holds the budget, locations and settings |
| **Ad group** | Sits inside a campaign, holds one tight theme of keywords and ads |

**Budget lives at campaign level.** You cannot set a budget on an ad group.

### Conversion vs click

| | |
|---|---|
| **Click** | Costs you money |
| **Conversion** | Makes you money |

A campaign with many clicks and no conversions is an expensive campaign, not a successful one.

### Broad, phrase and exact match

How loosely Google is allowed to match your keyword.

| Match type | Written as | How loose |
|---|---|---|
| **Broad** | `water filter` | Loosest — Google matches on meaning |
| **Phrase** | `"water filter"` | Middle — must include the meaning of your phrase |
| **Exact** | `[water filter]` | Tightest — very close to what you chose |

⚠️ **Exact match is not literally exact any more.** It includes close variations and rewordings. Lesson 3.3 explains what that means in practice.

## The Bidding Words

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Manual CPC** | You set bids yourself. Rare now. → Lesson 5.1 |
| **Smart Bidding** | Google sets each bid using its own signals. → Lesson 5.1 |
| **Maximise Conversions** | Get as many conversions as the budget allows. → Lesson 5.2 |
| **Target CPA (tCPA)** | Aim for a set cost per conversion. → Lesson 5.3 |
| **Target ROAS (tROAS)** | Aim for a set return on ad spend. → Lesson 5.3 |
| **Learning phase** | The period after a change while Google works things out. → Lesson 5.2 |

### The word that costs people the most money

⚠️ **Learning phase.**

After you change a bid strategy, budget or target, Google needs roughly two weeks to settle. Results during that time are unreliable.

**Most beginners change something, see bad numbers after three days, change it again, and never let anything finish learning.** Lesson 5.2 exists because this single mistake wastes more money than any other.

## The Words That Sound Similar But Are Opposites

Watch these carefully.

| Pair | The difference |
|---|---|
| **Negative keyword** vs **keyword** | A negative keyword **blocks** a search. A keyword **invites** one. |
| **Impression share lost to budget** vs **to rank** | Budget = you ran out of money. Rank = your ad was not good enough. Completely different fixes. → Lesson 8.1 |
| **Observation** vs **Targeting** | Observation = watch this group. Targeting = **only** show to this group. Choosing wrong can shrink your reach to almost nothing. → Lesson 6.7 |
| **Primary** vs **Secondary** conversion | Primary = Google chases it. Secondary = Google only reports it. → Lesson 2.3 |

⚠️ **The Observation / Targeting one is a genuine trap.** Picking "Targeting" when you meant "Observation" can cut your traffic by 90% overnight, and the setting is easy to miss.

## 📖 Real-World Example: The Word That Cost PKR 190,000

Bilal took over an account from a business that had been running ads for eight months.

The owner's complaint: **"We get lots of clicks but hardly any customers."**

### What he found

Their main campaign had a keyword: `water pump`.

It was set to **broad match**.

They had **no negative keywords at all**.

### What the search terms report showed

He opened the search terms report — the screen from Lesson 1.6 — and sorted by cost.

| What people actually typed | Cost | Sales |
|---|---|---|
| water pump repair | PKR 41,200 | 0 |
| water pump not working | PKR 28,600 | 0 |
| water pump motor price | PKR 22,400 | 1 |
| how to fix water pump | PKR 19,800 | 0 |
| water pump jobs | PKR 11,300 | 0 |
| **(actual buying searches)** | PKR 67,000 | 34 |

**They sold water pumps. They did not repair them.**

Roughly PKR 190,000 over eight months had gone to people looking for repairs, fixes, jobs and information.

### Why it happened

The owner did not know the difference between a **keyword** and a **search term**.

He had chosen the keyword `water pump` and believed that was what people would search. He had never opened the search terms report, because he did not know it existed or what it was for.

**He was not careless. He was missing one definition.**

### The fix

Twenty minutes of work:

- Added negative keywords: `repair`, `fix`, `broken`, `not working`, `jobs`, `how to`, `service`
- Changed the keyword from broad to phrase match
- Set a weekly reminder to check the search terms report

**Next month:** spend down 46%, sales up 31%.

### The point

⚠️ **This was not a strategy problem.** It was a vocabulary problem.

He did not need Smart Bidding, or Performance Max, or better ad copy. He needed to know that *keyword* and *search term* are two different things — which is one line in this lesson.

**That is why this lesson exists.** A missing definition is invisible. You do not know you are missing it, so you never look it up, and it quietly costs you money for eight months.

## 📌 Key Takeaways

- A keyword is what you bid on. A search term is what the person typed. The gap between them is where money leaks.
- ROAS is not profit. With a thin margin, a good-looking ROAS can still be a loss.
- Budget lives at campaign level, never at ad group level.
- Learning phase means waiting about two weeks after a change. Changing again too early is the most expensive beginner habit.
- Observation watches a group. Targeting restricts you to it. Confusing them can collapse your traffic overnight.
