## What Local Services Ads Are

**Local Services Ads (LSA)** appear right at the very top of Google search results, above even normal text ads, for local service searches.

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  LOCAL SERVICES ADS       ← very top     │
│  ✅ Google Guaranteed                    │
├─────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Normal text ads                         │
├─────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Map results                             │
├─────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Normal (free) search results            │
└─────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

They look different from text ads. They show your business name, your star rating, your review count, your hours, and a green **Google Guaranteed** badge.

⚠️ **Availability is limited.** LSA runs in certain countries and for certain job categories only. **At the time of writing it is not available in Pakistan.** Check Google's own LSA page for the current country and category list before planning anything around it.

**This lesson is here because:** if you work with clients in the US, UK, Canada or other supported markets — which many Pakistani freelancers and agencies do — LSA is often the single best-performing channel for local service businesses, and most people have never heard of it.

---

## The Big Difference: You Pay Per Lead

This is what makes LSA unlike everything else in this course.

| | Normal Google Ads | Local Services Ads |
|---|---|---|
| **You pay for** | A click | **A lead** |
| **A lead means** | — | A phone call or message from a real potential customer |
| **If nobody contacts you** | You still paid for clicks | **You pay nothing** |
| **Bad lead** | Your loss | **You can dispute it and get refunded** |
| **Keywords** | You choose them | **You do not choose any** |
| **Ad copy** | You write it | **You do not write any** |

### Why this matters

With normal Search ads, you pay for 100 clicks and hope some of them contact you.

With LSA, **you only pay when someone actually phones or messages you.** Clicks that go nowhere cost nothing.

⚠️ **This does not automatically make it cheaper.** A lead costs far more than a click. The question is whether cost *per lead* beats what you currently pay per lead through normal ads — which is exactly the maths from Lesson 0.3.

---

## What You Control, and What You Do Not

LSA gives you much less control than anything else in this course. That is the trade.

### ✅ You control

- Your service categories (what jobs you do)
- Your service area (where you work)
- Your weekly budget
- Your business hours
- Your profile: photos, description, licences
- Whether you pause

### ❌ You do not control

- Keywords
- Ad copy
- Headlines
- Landing pages
- Bids per search
- Position (Google decides)

**Google decides when to show you, based on your profile, your reviews, your responsiveness and your proximity to the searcher.**

---

## The Google Guaranteed Badge

The green badge is most of the value of LSA.

### What it means

Google has checked the business. Depending on category and country, that check can include a background check, licence checks and insurance checks.

**It also carries a guarantee for the customer.** If the customer is unhappy with the work, Google may refund what they paid, up to a limit Google sets.

### Why it matters commercially

⚠️ **For a stranger choosing a plumber, that badge is enormous.** They are letting someone into their home. A Google-backed check removes most of the fear that stops people booking.

**This is the same idea as Lesson 0.2:** find the fear and remove it. Google has built an entire product around one specific fear, and the badge is what you are really buying.

### What it costs you

Getting verified takes time — often two to six weeks — and requires real documents: licence, insurance, and in many categories a background check for the owner and sometimes staff.

**There is no way to shortcut this**, and that is the point. The barrier is what makes the badge worth something.

## Who Qualifies

LSA covers service jobs where someone comes to you or you go to them. Typical categories include:

| Home services | Professional services | Care services |
|---|---|---|
| Plumber | Lawyer | Childcare |
| Electrician | Real estate agent | Elder care |
| HVAC | Financial planner | Pet care |
| Roofer | Accountant | Tutoring |
| Locksmith | | |
| Cleaner | | |
| Pest control | | |

**The exact list changes and varies by country.** Check Google's current list rather than relying on this table.

❌ **Not for:** online shops, software, anything you cannot deliver locally in person.

## Setting It Up

### Step 1: Check you qualify

Country, category, and whether you hold the licences and insurance the category demands.

### Step 2: Create the profile

Business name, service areas, hours, categories, photos, and your licence details.

⚠️ **Your service area is important.** Too wide and you get leads you cannot serve. Too narrow and you get nothing. Lesson 9.3's thinking applies — map where your customers actually come from, not where you wish they came from.

### Step 3: Pass the checks

Submit licence, insurance and background checks. Then wait.

### Step 4: Set your weekly budget

LSA uses a weekly budget rather than daily. Google estimates how many leads that should produce.

**Start low.** You can raise it once you know your lead quality.

### Step 5: Get reviews

⚠️ **Reviews drive LSA ranking more than almost anything else.**

Google shows businesses with more and better reviews more often. If you have three reviews and a competitor has ninety, you will struggle regardless of budget.

**Build a habit:** ask every happy customer for a review, every time, and make it easy for them.

---

## Managing Leads

### Respond fast

**Response speed affects how often Google shows you.** It also affects whether you win the job — most people who contact three businesses hire whoever replies first.

Answer the phone. Reply to messages within minutes, not hours.

### Dispute bad leads

This is the feature most people never use, and it is real money.

**You can ask for a refund when a lead is genuinely not a customer.** Typical valid reasons:

| Reason | Example |
|---|---|
| **Wrong job type** | You do plumbing; they wanted electrical work |
| **Outside your area** | They are 200km away |
| **Spam or a bot** | Obvious junk |
| **A wrong number** | They meant to call someone else |
| **Someone selling to you** | A sales call, not a customer |

**Not valid:** the customer chose a competitor, or the price was too high, or they never replied afterwards. **Those are normal business losses, not bad leads.**

⚠️ **Dispute within the time window Google allows**, which is limited. Build it into your weekly routine or you will miss it.

**How much does this matter?** On a poorly-managed LSA account, 10–20% of leads can be disputable. On a PKR-equivalent spend of $3,000 a month, that is $300–600 you simply are not claiming.

## LSA Alongside Normal Google Ads

**They are not alternatives. Run both if you can.**

| | LSA | Search ads |
|---|---|---|
| Position | Very top | Below LSA |
| Payment | Per lead | Per click |
| Control | Almost none | Full |
| Trust signal | Google Guaranteed badge | Your own copy |
| Good for | Capturing ready-to-buy local demand | Everything else |

**Appearing in both means you occupy more of the screen** for the same search, which is a real advantage on mobile where very little fits above the fold.

⚠️ **Measure them separately.** They behave differently, cost differently, and should be judged differently. Do not blend them into one cost-per-lead number.

## 📖 Real-World Example: The Plumber Who Was Buying Leads Twice

Bilal picked up a UK client through his consultancy: a plumbing company in Manchester, spending £6,400 a month on Google Ads.

**Their problem:** cost per lead had risen from £34 to £71 over a year.

### What he found

Normal Search campaigns only. No LSA.

Their search terms were dominated by emergency phrases — `emergency plumber manchester`, `burst pipe help`, `plumber open now`. Competition on those was brutal, with cost per click above £12.

**And they were competing for position 3 or 4 on those searches** — because three LSA slots sat above every text ad.

### The finding that mattered

He ran the searches himself on a phone.

**On a mobile screen, the three LSA slots filled almost the entire first view.** The text ads — including his client's — needed a scroll to reach.

**They were paying £12 a click to be below the fold.**

### What he did

**Month 1: Applied for LSA.** Licence, public liability insurance, background checks. It took five weeks to get verified — slower than expected, which is normal.

**Month 1: Review campaign.** They had 11 reviews. He built a simple process: every completed job got a text message with a review link, sent the same day by the engineer. In eight weeks they went from 11 reviews to 78.

**Month 2: Launched LSA** at a small weekly budget, restricted to their genuine service area (not the whole of Greater Manchester, which they could not serve within an hour).

**Month 2: Rebalanced Search.** He moved budget away from the brutal emergency head terms, where LSA now covered them, and into longer, more specific searches where LSA did not appear — `boiler pressure loss`, `radiator not heating downstairs`.

**Month 3: Dispute routine.** A Friday-morning task: review every lead from the week and dispute the invalid ones. In the first month they recovered £412.

### Results at 6 months

| Metric | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly spend | £6,400 | £6,650 |
| — of which LSA | £0 | £2,900 |
| — of which Search | £6,400 | £3,750 |
| **Leads** | 90 | **214** |
| **Cost per lead** | **£71** | **£31** |
| Jobs booked | 38 | 97 |
| **Cost per booked job** | **£168** | **£69** |

**Roughly the same money. More than twice the jobs.**

### Why it worked

Three things, in order of size:

**1. Position.** They stopped fighting for a place below the fold and took a slot above it.

**2. Reviews.** Going from 11 to 78 reviews changed how often Google showed them. The review process cost nothing but a text message.

**3. Not competing with themselves.** Moving Search budget to terms LSA did not cover meant the two channels stopped bidding against each other.

### The part most people miss

⚠️ **The reviews did more than the budget.**

In month 2, with LSA live but only 23 reviews, they were getting few leads. By month 4, with 78 reviews and the same weekly budget, lead volume had nearly tripled.

**Nothing about the advertising changed in between.** The business just asked every customer for a review.

**That is a Lesson 0.1 finding again:** the biggest lever was outside the ad account.

## 📌 Key Takeaways

- Local Services Ads sit above all other ads and charge per lead, not per click. If nobody contacts you, you pay nothing.
- You control almost nothing: no keywords, no ad copy, no landing page. Google decides using your profile, reviews and response speed.
- The Google Guaranteed badge removes the fear that stops strangers booking a service. That is what you are really buying.
- Reviews drive LSA visibility more than budget does. Asking every customer costs nothing and changes everything.
- Dispute invalid leads every week. Most advertisers never do, and it is real money left unclaimed.

## 💪 Practical Exercise (30 minutes)

Work out whether LSA is worth it for a local service business.

1. Check whether their country and job category are supported. Use Google's current list.
2. Search their main service on a phone. Count how many LSA slots appear, and how far you must scroll to reach the text ads.
3. Count their reviews. Count their top three competitors' reviews. Write down the gap.
4. Work out their current cost per lead from normal Search ads.
5. Check whether they hold the licence and insurance the category demands.
6. Write a recommendation: apply now, fix reviews first, or not eligible.

## 🎯 Expert Challenge

Build a complete local-lead system that treats LSA as one part of it.

- Map the full local search result for your top ten service searches on mobile: LSA slots, text ads, map pack, organic. Work out what share of the first screen each takes, and where your client currently appears. **Most clients have never seen this and it changes the conversation immediately.**
- Design the review engine: who asks, when, by what message, and how it is tracked. Set a target review count based on the competitor gap, not a round number.
- Build the response-time measurement. Time from lead arriving to first human reply, measured weekly. This affects both LSA ranking and your win rate, and almost nobody measures it.
- Design the channel split: which searches LSA covers, which Search ads should cover, and how you stop them competing. Then measure them separately, never blended.
- Build the dispute routine as a recurring weekly task with a record of what was claimed and recovered.
- Finally, run the Lesson 0.3 maths properly: compare cost per *booked job* across LSA, Search and the map pack. Cost per lead is the vanity number here; cost per job that actually happened is the real one.
